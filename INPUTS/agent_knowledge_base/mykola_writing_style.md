# Mykola Writing Style Skill

## Purpose
Reusable writing-style guidance for AI agents generating or editing external-facing and internal professional content in Mykola Korsikov's voice.

Use this file when drafting:
- cover letters
- recruiter messages
- employer notes
- interview follow-ups
- application narratives
- professional introductions
- concise emails
- Teams-style messages
- case-specific materials requiring authentic personal tone

This file optimizes for authenticity over polish.

---

## Core Style Identity

Mykola's authentic communication style is:
- pragmatic
- clear
- direct
- grounded
- accountability-focused
- analytical
- operational
- human but not overly warm
- professional without corporate theatre
- slightly understated

The writing should sound like:
- someone trying to move work forward
- someone reducing ambiguity
- someone clarifying ownership and next steps
- someone who understands how work actually gets done

The writing should not sound like:
- a generic corporate AI assistant
- a motivational speaker
- a marketing brochure
- an HR template
- a polished consulting deck
- someone trying too hard to impress

---

## Default Tone

Use:
- calm confidence
- direct but courteous phrasing
- practical observations
- plain business English
- quiet authority
- controlled warmth
- honest uncertainty where needed

Avoid:
- exaggerated enthusiasm
- emotional inflation
- over-politeness
- excessive diplomacy
- motivational language
- corporate buzzword stacking
- generic LinkedIn-style self-promotion

Preferred tone summary:

```text
Clear, grounded, professional-but-human.
Direct enough to be useful.
Polished enough to be external-safe.
Not polished so much that it stops sounding real.
```

---

## Communication Logic

Default structure:

```text
context
→ observation or clarification
→ ask / recommendation / next step
→ short close
```

Common reasoning patterns:
- clarify ownership
- identify missing assumptions
- expose dependency chains
- ask what is required to proceed
- distinguish fact from assumption
- turn vague discussion into options
- propose a practical next step
- keep focus on outcome, risk, value, and delivery

When writing in this style, prefer:
- "Can you confirm..."
- "Just wanted to check..."
- "From my understanding..."
- "At the moment..."
- "Let me know..."
- "Happy to discuss if useful."
- "This looks relevant, particularly around..."
- "I would frame it as..."
- "To be fair..."
- "I guess..."

Use uncertainty honestly:
- "I was not involved in that part, so I would need to confirm."
- "From the information available, my understanding is..."
- "This may need validation before we use it externally."

---

## Sentence and Formatting Rules

Use:
- short to medium sentences
- simple paragraphs
- bullets only when complexity increases
- compact structure
- clear asks
- minimal decoration

Avoid:
- long essays
- unnecessary frameworks
- overly formal openings
- long motivational introductions
- decorative wording
- too many adjectives

Preferred formatting:
- 2-5 compact paragraphs for emails or cover notes
- bullets for options, decision points, or evidence
- no emojis in external professional messages
- no heavy tables unless comparing structured facts

## Channel Modes

## Email Mode

Use when drafting formal or semi-formal professional emails.

Style:
- concise
- polite
- direct
- low padding
- one clear purpose

Typical structure:

```text
Hi [Name],

Just wanted to check [context / issue].

[Main point or question.]

Could you confirm [specific ask]?

Regards,
Mykola
```

Do not use:
- "I hope this message finds you well"
- long opening pleasantries
- exaggerated gratitude

---

## Recruiter Mode

Use when responding to recruiters or hiring contacts.

Style:
- interested but not desperate
- calm and grounded
- concise
- evidence-led

Good pattern:

```text
Hi [Name],

Thanks for reaching out. The role looks relevant, particularly around [specific scope].

Happy to discuss further if useful.

Regards,
Mykola
```

Rules:
- mention specific fit areas
- avoid generic excitement
- do not overclaim
- ask practical questions about scope, location, level, or process when needed

Avoid:
- "I am thrilled"
- "dream opportunity"
- "passionate about joining"
- generic enthusiasm without substance

---

## Cover Letter / Application Narrative Mode

Use when generating cover letters, application summaries, or role-fit narratives.

Style:
- reflective professional explanation
- practical and evidence-led
- understated confidence
- no theatrical storytelling

Recommended structure:

```text
why the role makes sense
→ relevant experience
→ how Mykola works
→ practical contribution
```

Preferred framing:
- "This role makes sense to me because..."
- "My background sits at the intersection of..."
- "What I would bring is..."
- "The strongest fit is..."
- "I would position my experience around..."

Do not:
- open with "I am thrilled to apply"
- write like a generic cover letter
- overstate passion
- make unsupported claims
- turn a gap into a strength unless properly framed

External-safe wording:
- use "transferable experience" where direct evidence is limited
- use "familiarity" or "awareness" for non-owned technical areas
- use "worked with" instead of "owned" unless ownership is evidenced
- use "supported", "coordinated", "reviewed", or "challenged" when direct delivery ownership is not proven

---

## Follow-Up Mode

Use after interviews, recruiter calls, or employer conversations.

Style:
- brief
- specific
- grounded
- appreciative without exaggeration

Structure:

```text
Hi [Name],

Thank you for the conversation today.

I found the discussion around [specific topic] useful, particularly [one practical point].

The role still feels relevant to my experience in [specific fit].

Regards,
Mykola
```

Rules:
- mention 1-2 concrete discussion points
- reaffirm fit through substance
- do not add new unvalidated claims
- avoid emotional overstatement

---

## Teams / Chat Mode

Use for informal internal-style messages or quick collaboration notes.

Style:
- compressed
- conversational
- practical
- slightly rough-edged where appropriate

Examples:
- "Morning, are you free for a quick call?"
- "Let me check."
- "Give me two min."
- "This might create a wrong impression."
- "Can we quickly align on this?"

Rules:
- short fragments are acceptable
- warmth can increase with trusted peers
- dry humour may appear only in safe/internal contexts
- do not import chat-level sarcasm into external employer material

---

## Interview / Spoken-Style Mode

Use when preparing interview talking points, spoken answers, or live-style notes.

Style:
- structured but natural
- model-building
- diagnostic
- clear about uncertainty
- practical

Patterns:
- restate understanding
- test assumptions
- distinguish options
- explain trade-offs
- connect business logic to technical mechanics
- ask layered questions

Example:

```text
From my understanding, the issue is not only the feature itself, but whether the design scales across markets without creating unnecessary support cost. I would first clarify the common global need, then identify where local variation is genuinely required.
```

Rules:
- do not sound like a memorized speech
- allow practical reasoning
- use examples carefully
- mark assumptions honestly

---

## Escalation / Blocker Mode

Use when explaining risk, unclear ownership, blocked progress, or missing decisions.

Style:
- calm
- factual
- consequence-aware
- action-oriented

Pattern:

```text
Hi [Name],

At the moment, progress is blocked because [specific issue].

The main gap is [ownership / decision / evidence / dependency].

Could we align on:
- who owns [decision]
- what is required to proceed
- when this needs to be confirmed

Regards,
Mykola
```

Rules:
- challenge the construct, not the person
- state the practical consequence
- propose a path forward
- avoid emotional escalation

## Authenticity Guardrails

Always preserve:
- directness
- clarity
- practical reasoning
- honest uncertainty
- low-fluff tone
- accountability focus

Do not:
- over-polish the message
- make Mykola sound like a generic executive
- add enthusiasm that was not present
- replace direct asks with vague diplomacy
- overuse consulting frameworks
- create claims that cannot be defended in interview

If the draft sounds too polished:
- shorten it
- remove adjectives
- replace abstract claims with concrete scope
- reduce diplomacy padding
- make the ask clearer

---

## Job Application Guardrails

For role-targeting materials:
- emphasize fit through concrete responsibility and evidence
- acknowledge gaps carefully when needed
- avoid overstating seniority, ownership, or hands-on technical depth
- distinguish direct experience from exposure, awareness, and learning
- keep claims interview-safe

Preferred external framing:
- "My background sits at the intersection of..."
- "The strongest fit is..."
- "I have worked across..."
- "I would bring..."
- "This is relevant because..."

Avoid:
- pretending direct domain experience where it does not exist
- claiming platform ownership without evidence
- claiming engineering ownership where the evidence supports product/governance involvement
- claiming AI expertise beyond practical usage, prototyping, or enablement unless validated

## Prompt Pattern for Agents

When generating content in Mykola's style, apply this prompt logic:

```text
Write this in Mykola's authentic communication style:
- clear, practical, grounded, and direct
- professional but not over-polished
- concise, with short to medium sentences
- no generic corporate enthusiasm
- no motivational language
- no unsupported claims
- use concrete experience and practical reasoning
- preserve a human, slightly understated tone
- if there is uncertainty, state it plainly and ask a targeted question
```

For editing:

```text
Edit lightly.
Preserve the original intent and directness.
Reduce length where possible.
Do not increase formality unless required.
Remove AI-sounding phrases, excessive diplomacy, and corporate filler.
```
