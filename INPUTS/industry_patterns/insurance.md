# Insurance Industry Pattern

## Industry Characteristics

* Risk- and policy-centric operating model
* High dependency on underwriting, claims, actuarial, regulatory, and distribution capabilities
* Complex product, policy, customer, broker, and claims data structures
* Significant legacy core platform dependency
* Document-heavy workflows and high manual decision support needs
* Strong regulatory, privacy, solvency, and conduct obligations
* Transformation pressure from digital experience, cost-to-serve, fraud, and modernization of core systems

---

## Common Architecture Characteristics

* Legacy policy administration and claims platforms with high customization
* Product-line-specific systems and fragmented customer views
* Heavy batch integrations between policy, billing, claims, finance, and reporting
* Document-centric workflows across underwriting, claims, and compliance
* Digital portals layered over constrained core systems
* Data platforms used to compensate for core system reporting limitations

---

## Data Characteristics

* Complex customer, policy, product, risk, claims, broker, and finance data
* High dependency on third-party data for underwriting, fraud, and claims decisions
* Strong auditability and explainability needs for pricing, underwriting, and claims actions
* Long retention periods for policies, claims, and regulatory evidence
* Data quality challenges from mergers, legacy migrations, and product variation
* Real-time needs for digital quoting, claims status, and customer service

---

## Governance & Regulatory Constraints

* Insurance conduct and consumer protection regulations
* Solvency and capital reporting obligations
* Data privacy and consent requirements
* Model governance for pricing, underwriting, fraud, and AI decision support
* Auditability and explainability for automated decisions
* Segregation of duties for finance and claims controls
* Cybersecurity controls for customer, policy, and claims data
* Regional regulatory variation across markets and product lines
