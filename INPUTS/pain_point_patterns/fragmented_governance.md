# Fragmented Governance

## Summary
Fragmented governance occurs when decision rights, standards, ownership, and controls are split across teams without clear enterprise alignment.

## Symptoms
- Different teams apply different standards for similar decisions
- Architecture, data, process, and security governance operate separately
- Ownership of cross-functional issues is unclear
- Decisions are revisited repeatedly without traceability
- Exceptions become the normal operating model

## Typical Root Causes
- Siloed organizational structure
- Weak enterprise governance forums
- Lack of decision rights and escalation paths
- Inconsistent standards and policy enforcement
- Local budget ownership overriding enterprise priorities

## Business Impact
- Slow decision-making and transformation delivery
- Increased operational, compliance, and technology risk
- Duplicated investments and inconsistent outcomes
- Lower trust in enterprise standards
- Difficulty scaling change across functions or regions

## Architecture Implications
- Architecture standards are not consistently enforced
- Data and process ownership is unclear
- Integrations and systems evolve without shared guardrails
- Governance is disconnected from delivery workflows
- Operating model lacks clear accountability

## Typical KPIs and Evidence Signals
- decision cycle time
- number of policy exceptions
- repeat governance escalations
- architecture review bypass rate
- duplicated investment count
- audit findings linked to unclear ownership

## Common Solution Directions
- Define decision rights and governance forums
- Establish enterprise standards and exception processes
- Connect governance to delivery workflows
- Create accountable owners for cross-functional domains
- Use dashboards to track decisions, exceptions, and risks

## Risks and Watchouts
- Creating governance bureaucracy without decision speed
- Over-centralizing decisions that need local ownership
- Weak executive sponsorship
- Poor adoption of standards
- Treating governance as documentation rather than behavior change
