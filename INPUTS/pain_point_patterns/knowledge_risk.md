# Knowledge Risk

## Summary
Knowledge risk occurs when critical business, process, system, or transformation knowledge is undocumented, hard to find, or concentrated in a small number of people.

## Symptoms
- Key activities depend on specific SMEs
- Documentation is outdated, missing, or scattered
- New team members take a long time to become productive
- Incidents require informal expert networks to resolve
- Process rationale and system behavior are poorly understood

## Typical Root Causes
- Tribal knowledge culture
- Weak documentation ownership
- Legacy systems with limited documentation
- High turnover or reliance on contractors
- Fragmented knowledge repositories and search

## Business Impact
- Higher operational continuity risk
- Slower onboarding and change delivery
- Increased support and incident resolution time
- Reduced transformation confidence
- Loss of institutional knowledge during role changes

## Architecture Implications
- System and process knowledge is not captured in architecture repositories
- Dependencies are discovered late in change programs
- Data and integration logic may be undocumented
- Governance decisions lack historical context
- Operating model overdepends on individual experts

## Commonly Affected Business Capabilities
- knowledge management
- service management
- enterprise architecture management
- process management
- training and onboarding
- operational resilience

## Commonly Affected Technology Capabilities
- knowledge management platform
- enterprise search
- document management
- architecture repository management
- service knowledge management
- access control management

## Relevant AI and Automation Opportunities
- knowledge assistant for approved repositories
- AI copilot for documentation creation
- intelligent document processing to classify knowledge assets
- AI agent orchestration for expert handoffs
- AI-assisted reporting of knowledge gaps

## Relevant Value Models
- operational_efficiency
- governance_maturity
- ai_adoption_value

## Typical KPIs and Evidence Signals
- onboarding time
- incident resolution dependency on named SMEs
- documentation freshness
- knowledge article reuse rate
- number of critical undocumented processes
- SME query volume

## Common Solution Directions
- Define knowledge ownership and lifecycle
- Create curated knowledge repositories
- Capture architecture and process decisions
- Implement knowledge assistant with source grounding
- Include knowledge transfer in transformation plans

## Risks and Watchouts
- Capturing volume without quality
- Exposing outdated or unapproved knowledge through AI
- Ignoring access controls
- Failing to reward documentation behavior
- Treating knowledge management as a one-time cleanup
