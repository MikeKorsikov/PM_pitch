# Low Automation

## Summary
Low automation occurs when routine, rules-based, or repeatable work remains manual despite being suitable for workflow, integration, robotic, or AI-enabled automation.

## Symptoms
- Users manually copy data between systems
- Repetitive approvals and status updates are handled by email
- Teams rely on checklists and spreadsheets for recurring tasks
- Error-prone tasks consume significant operational capacity
- Automation exists only in isolated pockets

## Typical Root Causes
- Fragmented systems and poor integration
- Weak process standardization
- Lack of automation strategy or ownership
- Poor data quality and inconsistent rules
- Short-term workarounds replacing durable process design

## Business Impact
- Higher operating cost and slower execution
- Increased error and rework rates
- Poor employee experience from repetitive work
- Limited scalability without adding headcount
- Slower transformation benefit realization

## Architecture Implications
- Manual steps compensate for missing integrations
- Workflow logic is not embedded in platforms
- Automation opportunities are hard to prioritize without process visibility
- Governance may not track local scripts and bots
- Operating model remains task-heavy instead of exception-based

- workflow automation for routing and approvals

- intelligent document processing for intake tasks
- AI copilot for analyst productivity

## Typical KPIs and Evidence Signals
- manual effort hours
- automation rate
- transaction cycle time
- error and rework rate
- cost per transaction
- number of manual handoffs

## Common Solution Directions
- Identify high-volume repeatable tasks
- Standardize process rules before automation
- Prioritize automation by value and feasibility
- Integrate systems through APIs or workflow tools
- Establish automation governance and ownership

## Risks and Watchouts
- Automating unstable or poorly understood processes
- Creating unmanaged bot sprawl
- Ignoring exception handling
- Overstating AI value where simple automation is enough
- Lack of business ownership after deployment
