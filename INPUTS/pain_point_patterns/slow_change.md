# Slow Change

## Summary
Slow change occurs when the organization cannot deliver process, system, product, regulatory, or operating model changes at the pace required by business needs.

## Symptoms
- Small changes take months to approve and deliver
- Release cycles are slow and heavily manual
- Stakeholders avoid change due to perceived risk
- Transformation programs spend excessive time on alignment
- Change backlogs grow faster than delivery capacity

## Typical Root Causes
- Legacy systems and tightly coupled architecture
- Fragmented governance and decision rights
- Manual testing, validation, and approval processes
- Poor documentation and knowledge dependency
- Excessive customization and local process variation

## Business Impact
- Missed market, regulatory, or operational opportunities
- Higher transformation cost and lower benefit realization
- Increased frustration among business stakeholders
- Delayed modernization and AI adoption
- Reduced competitiveness and adaptability

## Architecture Implications
- Systems are difficult to modify safely
- Change impact analysis is slow due to weak documentation
- Integration dependencies create high regression risk
- Governance and delivery workflows are not streamlined
- Operating model lacks agile decision paths

## Typical KPIs and Evidence Signals
- change lead time
- release frequency
- approval cycle time
- change failure rate
- backlog aging
- time to complete impact assessment

## Common Solution Directions
- Simplify governance and decision rights
- Decouple architecture and reduce dependencies
- Automate testing and release controls
- Improve documentation and impact analysis
- Prioritize change portfolio by value and risk

## Risks and Watchouts
- Speeding up delivery without control design
- Blaming governance when architecture is the bottleneck
- Ignoring regulated validation requirements
- Underinvesting in change adoption
- Overusing AI-generated impact analysis without expert review
