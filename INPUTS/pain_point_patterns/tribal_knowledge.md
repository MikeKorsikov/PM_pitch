# Tribal Knowledge

## Summary
Tribal knowledge occurs when important knowledge about processes, systems, decisions, or exceptions exists mainly in people's heads rather than governed repositories.

## Symptoms
- Only a few people know how critical processes really work
- Work instructions differ from actual practice
- Teams rely on informal networks to solve issues
- Departures or role changes create operational risk
- Documentation lacks exception handling and rationale

## Typical Root Causes
- Weak documentation culture and ownership
- Complex legacy processes and systems
- High reliance on long-tenured SMEs
- Lack of structured knowledge management
- Fast delivery pressure with limited time for capture

## Business Impact
- Operational continuity risk
- Slower onboarding and training
- Longer incident and problem resolution
- Reduced transformation confidence
- Increased risk during outsourcing, reorganization, or retirement

## Architecture Implications
- Current-state architecture is incomplete
- Business rules and exceptions are not captured in systems
- Dependency analysis relies on interviews
- Knowledge repositories are fragmented or outdated
- Operating model is fragile under staff changes

## Commonly Affected Business Capabilities
- knowledge management
- training and onboarding
- service management
- process management
- operational resilience
- enterprise architecture management

## Commonly Affected Technology Capabilities
- knowledge base management
- enterprise search
- documentation management
- architecture repository management
- collaboration platform management

## Relevant AI and Automation Opportunities
- knowledge assistant with approved source grounding
- AI copilot for documentation drafting
- intelligent document processing for knowledge extraction
- AI-assisted interview summarization for SME capture
- workflow automation for knowledge review cycles

## Relevant Value Models
- operational_efficiency
- governance_maturity
- ai_adoption_value

## Typical KPIs and Evidence Signals
- number of critical SME dependencies
- documentation completeness
- onboarding time
- repeated questions to SMEs
- incident resolution time
- knowledge article reuse rate

## Common Solution Directions
- Identify critical knowledge domains and SME risks
- Capture process, system, and exception knowledge
- Assign knowledge ownership and review cycles
- Build searchable, governed repositories
- Use AI assistants only on curated sources

## Risks and Watchouts
- Capturing outdated or unofficial practices as truth
- Overloading SMEs with documentation tasks
- Weak access controls over sensitive knowledge
- No maintenance after initial capture
- AI answers without source citations
