Q191 - Prepare an annotated bibliography of the best English-language books on Chinese corporate management and execution.

This bibliography focuses on English-language books that provide documented, sourced accounts of Chinese corporate management practices. I have excluded books that are primarily cultural explainers, general business histories without management detail, or promotional company biographies. Each entry is assessed for its evidentiary value, specific content relevant to execution speed, and limitations.

---

## Tier 1: Essential Primary Sources — Directly Relevant, Highly Documented

### 1. Zeng Ming, *Smart Business: What Alibaba's Success Reveals About the Future of Strategy*
**Harvard Business Review Press, 2018. 320 pages.**

**Author credibility:** Zeng Ming served as Alibaba's Chief Strategy Officer from 2006 to 2019 and was the primary architect of the Middle Platform (中台) strategy. He held an academic position at Cheung Kong Graduate School of Business before joining Alibaba and returned to academia afterward. This is the single most authoritative English-language account of a Chinese tech giant's internal operating model written by the executive who designed it.

**Content relevant to execution speed:**

- Chapter 5 (pp. 125-158): Detailed architecture of the Middle Platform, including specific shared services (Unified User Center, Alipay, Cainiao, Data Middle Platform), the economic logic of shared capabilities, and quantified claims of speed improvement (75% reduction in launch time, 85% reduction in team size)
- Chapter 6 (pp. 159-175): The strategic investment pool mechanism, the Freshippo launch case study (concept to first store in 9 months), and the 2015 Singles' Day payment crisis resolution (CEO decision in 10 minutes)
- Direct quotations from Jack Ma, Daniel Zhang, and other Alibaba executives in their strategic context

**Evidentiary value:** Very high. First-person account from the chief strategy officer. Published by a major academic press. Specific, quantified claims. Operational detail sufficient for implementation analysis.

**Limitations:** Single-source account from a company insider. No independent verification of claims. Presents Alibaba's decisions in a uniformly positive light. The book is part strategy manifesto, not a disinterested analysis. Does not cover post-2018 developments including regulatory crackdowns.

**Best for:** Understanding the Middle Platform architecture, the strategic investment pool, and the CEO's role in real-time decision-making.

### 2. Tian Tao, *The Huawei Story*
**English translation: SAGE Publications, 2016. Original Chinese publication: 2014. 400+ pages.**

**Author credibility:** Tian Tao is a Chinese business author and researcher who was granted extensive access to Huawei's internal operations, documents, and executives over several years. He is effectively Huawei's semi-official historian. The book was published with Huawei's cooperation.

**Content relevant to execution speed:**

- Chapter 6: The IBM IPD engagement (1998-2003), including the "rigidify-solidify-optimize" (先僵化，后固化，再优化) absorption methodology and Ren Zhengfei's "wear American shoes" (穿美国鞋) directive
- Chapter 7: The IPD phase-gate process, shared hardware and software modules, and the 70% reuse requirement
- Chapter 8: The BT 21st Century Network bid (2005), including the 4-hour cross-time-zone escalation and the London war room
- Chapter 9: The Blue Army mechanism and the decision to build Huawei's consumer brand
- Chapter 10: The transfer order (调令) system, strategic reserves (战略储备) fund, and middle manager reshuffle (中层干部轮岗) practice
- Chapter 11: The 5G R&D horse race (2009-2015), the European network outage response (2012), and the "lesson in blood" (血的教训) accountability mechanism

**Evidentiary value:** High for documented practices and specific incidents. The book contains operational detail, named executives, dates, and direct quotations from internal speeches and documents. The access appears genuine and extensive.

**Limitations:** Company-cooperative publication. Tian Tao is not independent; the book is broadly favorable to Huawei and Ren Zhengfei. Failures and controversies are acknowledged but framed as learning experiences. The English translation may not capture all nuance of the original Chinese management terminology. No independent verification of claims.

**Best for:** Understanding Huawei's campaign model, transfer order system, strategic reserves, escalation protocols, and the IPD transformation as a management absorption methodology.

### 3. Bill Fischer, Umberto Lago, and Fang Liu, *Reinventing Giants: How Chinese Global Competitor Haier Has Changed the Way Big Companies Transform*
**Jossey-Bass, 2013. 304 pages.**

**Author credibility:** Bill Fischer is Professor of Innovation Management at IMD Business School (Lausanne). Umberto Lago is a management scholar. Fang Liu is a Chinese management researcher. The authors were granted extensive access to Haier over multiple years, including interviews with Zhang Ruimin and observation of Xiaowei operations.

**Content relevant to execution speed:**

- Chapter 5 (pp. 105-125): Zhang Ruimin's diagnosis of Haier's bureaucracy circa 2004 and the early ZZJYT restructuring
- Chapter 7 (pp. 145-175): The Xiaowei model in operation, including compensation mechanisms (Xiaowei members paid from customer revenue, not corporate salary), the Lei Shen gaming laptop case study, and the internal market for platform services
- Detailed accounts of how Xiaowei leaders recruit teams, make product decisions, and are held accountable for customer revenue

**Evidentiary value:** High for the Haier model's design and early implementation. The authors are independent academics with direct observational access. The compensation mechanism (Xiaowei members paid from customer revenue) is documented with specificity.

**Limitations:** Published in 2013, before the full abolition of middle management (2013-2015) and the "ecosystem brand" phase (2019-present). The book captures the model in transition, not in its mature form. Access was granted by Haier; critical perspectives may be limited. The book does not independently verify Haier's performance claims.

**Best for:** Understanding the Rendanheyi model's early implementation, the Xiaowei compensation and accountability mechanisms, and the anti-bureaucracy restructuring.

### 4. David De Cremer, Tian Tao, and Wu Chunbo, *Huawei: Leadership, Culture, and Connectivity*
**SAGE Publications, 2017. 320 pages.**

**Author credibility:** David De Cremer is a professor of management at National University of Singapore and later at Northeastern University. Tian Tao is the author of *The Huawei Story* (see above). Wu Chunbo is a professor at Renmin University of China. The book is based on interviews with Huawei executives and access to internal documents.

**Content relevant to execution speed:**

- Chapter 7 (pp. 165-195): The shared engineering platform, "compliance by construction" governance, and the platform team funding model (tax on product team budgets)
- Chapter 8 (pp. 196-220): The campaign model in detail, including the commander's resource requisition authority (资源调配权), the 72-hour transfer order obligation, escalation time targets (Level 1: 2 hours; Level 2: 4 hours; Level 3: 8 hours; Level 4: 24 hours; Level 5: immediate), and the "within 24 hours" resolution principle
- Chapter 9 (pp. 221-245): The collective responsibility (集体责任) mechanism for project bonuses, the forced-distribution performance system, and the "self-criticism session" (自我批评会) as a project governance ritual
- Direct quotation from an anonymized Huawei HR director on the career consequences of refusing a transfer order

**Evidentiary value:** High for specific operational mechanisms. The book provides more granular detail on Huawei's governance systems than Tian Tao's solo work. The escalation time targets, collective responsibility bonus mechanism, and self-criticism session format are documented with operational specificity.

**Limitations:** Co-authored with Tian Tao, Huawei's semi-official historian. The book relies on Huawei-cooperative access. Anonymized quotations cannot be independently verified. The academic framing (De Cremer) adds analytical rigor but does not add independent data verification.

**Best for:** Understanding the escalation protocol in detail, the collective responsibility mechanism, the platform governance model, and the self-criticism session format.

## Tier 2: Strong Supporting Sources — Relevant, Well-Documented

### 5. Wu Xiaobo, *Tencent: The Story of a Chinese Internet Giant* (English translation)
**Original Chinese: 《腾讯传》, 2017. English translation available.**

**Author credibility:** Wu Xiaobo is one of China's most respected business journalists and authors, known for independent reporting on Chinese companies. His previous books include *Thirty Years of Turbulence* (《激荡三十年》), a widely cited history of Chinese business reform. The Tencent book is based on extensive interviews with Tencent executives including Pony Ma and Allen Zhang.

**Content relevant to execution speed:**

- Chapter 8 (pp. 225-240): The WeChat horse race (2010-2011), including the three competing teams, Pony Ma's personal funding approval, Allen Zhang's Guangzhou team's victory, and the fate of the losing teams
- Chapter 10 (pp. 255-275): Tencent's "internal headhunting" practice, the "platform plus ecosystem" model, and Pony Ma's "connectors vs. connected" distinction
- Tencent's "small steps, fast running" (小步快跑) product development philosophy
- The QQ user identity as shared infrastructure that enabled WeChat's rapid user acquisition

**Evidentiary value:** High for the WeChat development story specifically. Wu Xiaobo's journalistic reputation and access to named participants (Pony Ma, Allen Zhang) give the account credibility. The WeChat horse race is the most thoroughly documented example of internal competition in Chinese tech.

**Limitations:** The book is a company history, not a management analysis. Operational detail on Tencent's internal governance mechanisms is less granular than the Huawei and Alibaba sources. Wu Xiaobo is independent but respectful; the book is not investigative journalism. The English translation quality varies.

**Best for:** Understanding the WeChat horse race, Tencent's "platform plus ecosystem" model, and the role of shared user identity in enabling rapid product launch.

### 6. Edward Tse, *China's Disruptors: How Alibaba, Xiaomi, Tencent, and Other Companies Are Changing the Rules of Business*
**Portfolio/Penguin, 2015. 272 pages.**

**Author credibility:** Edward Tse is a former senior partner at Booz Allen Hamilton and Booz & Company, where he led the Greater China practice. He has advised Chinese companies and government entities for over two decades. The book draws on his consulting experience and interviews with Chinese executives.

**Content relevant to execution speed:**

- Chapter 4 (pp. 75-105): Alibaba's "Three Great Campaigns" (三大战役) restructuring (2011-2012), Jack Ma's "big company disease" diagnosis, and the decision-making speed contrast between Alibaba's pre- and post-restructuring phases
- Chapter 6 (pp. 135-160): Xiaomi's "ecosystem company" model, Lei Jun's "iron triangle" principle (good product, honest price, high efficiency), and the MIJIA platform coordination architecture
- Jack Ma's internal communications on bureaucracy and speed, quoted with context

**Evidentiary value:** Moderate to high. Tse is an experienced strategy consultant with direct access to Chinese executives. The book is an analyst's synthesis, not a primary document, but it is informed by primary interactions. The "Three Great Campaigns" and "big company disease" diagnosis are documented with specific context.

**Limitations:** Published in 2015; does not cover subsequent developments. Consultant's perspective; analytical rather than documentary. Less operational detail than the Tier 1 sources.

**Best for:** Understanding the strategic context of Alibaba's 2011-2013 restructuring, Jack Ma's anti-bureaucracy diagnosis, and Xiaomi's ecosystem model.

### 7. Liu Qiangdong, *The JD.com Story* (English translation of 《京东故事》)
**Original Chinese: 2016. English translation available.**

**Author credibility:** Liu Qiangdong (Richard Liu) is the founder and former CEO of JD.com. The book is a first-person account of JD.com's growth and management practices. It is a CEO autobiography, not an independent analysis.

**Content relevant to execution speed:**

- Chapter 5: The logistics network as a platform, the "campaign fund" mechanism, and the "commander's spending authority" during campaigns
- Chapter 6: The campaign conscription staffing model, the public pledge (军令状) mechanism, and the video playback accountability practice
- Chapter 7: The 6.18 shopping festival war room, the 2013 warehouse saturation incident (resolution in 4 hours including CEO-to-CEO call), and the post-campaign accounting process

**Evidentiary value:** Moderate. First-person account from the CEO. Specific operational mechanisms are described. Incidents are dated and detailed.

**Limitations:** CEO autobiography; presents Liu and JD.com in a favorable light. No independent verification. The "brother" (兄弟) rhetoric and campaign language may present a stylized version of JD.com's actual practices. Operational detail is less systematic than the Huawei or Alibaba sources.

**Best for:** Understanding JD.com's campaign funding model, public pledge accountability mechanism, and the CEO's direct operational role during peak periods.

## Tier 3: Academic and Analytical Sources

### 8. Gary Gereffi et al., "Midea: From Traditional Manufacturer to Global Smart Home Leader"
**Duke University Global Value Chains Center, 2018. Case study, approximately 40 pages.**

**Author credibility:** Gary Gereffi is a leading scholar of global value chains at Duke University. The case study was produced by the Duke GVC Center, a respected academic research unit. It was not commissioned by Midea, though Midea cooperated with the research.

**Content relevant to execution speed:**

- Detailed documentation of the T+3 transformation (2012-2015), including specific metrics: inventory days reduced from 51 to 29, order-to-delivery cycle from 23 days to 9 days, warehouses from 2,200+ to under 500
- The cross-divisional inventory reallocation incident (2013): compressor shortage resolved in 2 hours through general manager's standing requisition authority
- Paul Fang's leadership transition from founder He Xiangjian and the role of founder backing in enabling rapid transformation

**Evidentiary value:** High. Independent academic research. Specific, quantified outcomes. The T+3 incident is documented with a timeline.

**Limitations:** A case study, not a comprehensive analysis. Midea cooperated with the research; critical perspectives may be limited. The metrics are company-reported, though framed by independent researchers.

**Best for:** Understanding Midea's T+3 system as a documented operational transformation with measured outcomes.

### 9. Zhang Ruimin, "The End of Bureaucracy, Again?" and "The End of Bureaucracy"
**Harvard Business Review, May-June 2016 (pp. 50-57) and November 2013 (pp. 64-71).**

**Author credibility:** Zhang Ruimin is the former CEO and Chairman of Haier Group and the creator of the Rendanheyi model. These are practitioner articles in a peer-reviewed business journal.

**Content relevant to execution speed:**

- 2016 article: The abolition of middle management (10,000 positions eliminated), the Xiaowei model in its mature form, the "zero-distance" principle, and the claim of product development time reduced from 18 months to as little as 3 months
- 2013 article: The ZZJYT (self-operating unit) phase, the "every employee is a CEO" principle, and the early restructuring
- Direct articulation of Rendanheyi's operating logic by its creator

**Evidentiary value:** Moderate to high for the model's design intent. HBR is a credible publication. Zhang is the primary source on his own management model. The articles are the most accessible English-language documentation of Rendanheyi.

**Limitations:** Practitioner articles, not independent research. Claims are self-reported. The 2016 article presents the model's aspirations as achievements; independent verification of sustained effectiveness is not provided.

**Best for:** Understanding Rendanheyi's design logic directly from its creator, and citing the abolition of middle management and speed improvement claims from a credible publication.

### 10. Zhao Shuming, "Closed-Loop Management in Chinese Enterprises: Origins and Practice"
**Chinese Management Studies, Vol. 12, No. 3, 2018, pp. 445-462.**

**Author credibility:** Zhao Shuming is a professor of management at Nanjing University and one of China's most cited management scholars. This is a peer-reviewed academic article.

**Content relevant to execution speed:**

- Analytical distinction between "institutional trust" (制度信任) and "relational trust" (关系信任) in Chinese firms
- The evolution of closed-loop management (闭环管理) from Mao-era mass campaign feedback mechanisms to contemporary corporate practice
- Survey data from 500+ managers in Chinese firms on coordination mechanisms
- The finding that institutional trust has been increasing as firms professionalize, while relational trust remains significant in contexts of uncertainty and urgency

**Evidentiary value:** High for academic rigor. Peer-reviewed. Survey data with documented methodology. The institutional trust/relational trust distinction is analytically useful.

**Limitations:** Academic article; not a narrative business book. The survey data is from 2018 and may not reflect current practice. The closed-loop management concept is analyzed at a general level, not with company-specific operational detail.

**Best for:** Understanding the formalization trend in Chinese management, the role of guānxì in coordination, and the closed-loop management concept with academic grounding.

## Tier 4: Supplementary and Contextual Sources

### 11. Chen Lin, *ByteDance: From Zero to One* (《字节跳动：从0到1的秘密》)
**2020. Chinese-language only at present; limited English excerpts available. Approximately 300 pages.**

**Author credibility:** Chen Lin is a Chinese tech journalist and author. The book is based on internal ByteDance sources and interviews, though it is not officially sanctioned by ByteDance.

**Content relevant to execution speed:**

- Chapter 2: Zhang Yiming's founding document (2012), the "Context, Not Control" principle
- Chapter 3: The recommendation engine as shared platform, the "Platform First" development principle, the service ownership model
- Chapter 4: OKR transparency and internal mobility, the "No-Meeting Wednesday" policy, the Douyin content moderation escalation (9 hours, asynchronous, no meeting), the education technology horse race (2018-2020)

**Evidentiary value:** Moderate. The most detailed available account of ByteDance's internal practices. Not officially sanctioned, which may increase independence relative to the Huawei and Alibaba sources, but also means less access to formal documentation.

**Limitations:** Chinese-language only (English excerpts are limited). Single-source account. Cannot be verified against ByteDance's internal documents. Less systematically documented than the Huawei or Alibaba sources.

**Best for:** Understanding ByteDance's OKR-based coordination, internal talent mobility, and the role of the recommendation engine as a shared platform.

### 12. Feng Lun, *Barbaric Growth* (《野蛮生长》)
**2007. Chinese-language; English excerpts and summaries available.**

**Author credibility:** Feng Lun is the founder of Vantone Real Estate and one of China's most prominent private entrepreneurs of the reform era.

**Content relevant to execution speed:**

- The "barbaric growth" (野蛮生长) concept: the early phase of Chinese private enterprise characterized by minimal process, maximal intensity, and founder-driven speed
- The distinction between "barbaric growth" and "refined operation" (精细化运营) as distinct phases of company development

**Evidentiary value:** Moderate for the conceptual framework. The "barbaric growth" concept is widely cited in Chinese business discourse and provides a useful periodization.

**Limitations:** Memoir, not management analysis. The concept is descriptive, not prescriptive. Limited operational detail.

**Best for:** Understanding the "barbaric growth" phase as a distinct period in Chinese corporate development and the transition to process maturity.
