Q192 - Prepare a separate bibliography of Chinese-language books written by or about Huawei, Alibaba, Tencent, Haier, BYD, Xiaomi and ByteDance.

This bibliography covers Chinese-language books that document management practices, execution systems, and corporate transformations at the seven firms. I have organized entries by company, with assessments of author credibility, access level, and evidentiary value. Where authors are company insiders, semi-official historians, or independent journalists, I note the distinction explicitly.

## HUAWEI (华为)

### 1. Tian Tao (田涛), *The Huawei Story* (《华为故事》)
**CITIC Press (中信出版社), 2014. Updated edition 2016. Approximately 450 pages.**

**Author status:** Semi-official historian. Tian Tao was granted multi-year access to Huawei's internal operations, documents, and executives. He co-founded the Huawei International Advisory Council and has written multiple books on the company with Huawei's explicit cooperation.

**Content:** The most comprehensive single-volume account of Huawei's management transformation. Covers the IBM IPD engagement (1998-2003), the "rigidify-solidify-optimize" (先僵化，后固化，再优化) absorption methodology, the Blue Army (蓝军) mechanism, the transfer order (调令) system, campaign governance, the BT 21st Century Network bid (2005), the 5G R&D horse race, and the European network outage response (2012). Contains direct quotations from Ren Zhengfei's internal speeches and documents.

**Evidentiary value:** Very high for documented practices. Specific dates, named executives, internal document references, and operational detail. The access is genuine and extensive. The book is the foundation for most subsequent English-language writing on Huawei's management.

**Limitations:** Company-cooperative. Tian Tao is not independent; the book presents Huawei and Ren Zhengfei favorably. Controversies are framed as learning experiences. The book is a semi-official history, not investigative journalism.

---

### 2. Tian Tao (田涛) and Wu Chunbo (吴春波), *The Next Fall Will Not Be Huawei* (《下一个倒下的会不会是华为》)
**CITIC Press (中信出版社), 2012. Updated edition 2017. Approximately 380 pages.**

**Author status:** Tian Tao (see above). Wu Chunbo is a professor at Renmin University of China and a long-time Huawei adviser who served on Huawei's advisory board.

**Content:** Focuses on Huawei's crisis management philosophy, Ren Zhengfei's leadership principles, and the self-criticism culture. The title references Ren's famous question: "Will Huawei fall next?" Documents the "grayscale" (灰度) leadership philosophy, the "Red Army vs. Blue Army" internal opposition system, and Huawei's response to successive crises. Less operational detail than *The Huawei Story* but more strategic and philosophical depth.

**Evidentiary value:** High for Ren Zhengfei's management philosophy and Huawei's crisis response mechanisms. Wu Chunbo's advisory role provides additional access and perspective.

**Limitations:** Same limitations as Tian Tao's solo work: company-cooperative, favorable presentation. More philosophical than operational.

---

### 3. Cheng Dong (程东), *The Truth About Huawei* (《华为真相》)
**CITIC Press (中信出版社), 2010. Approximately 350 pages.**

**Author status:** Independent business journalist. Cheng Dong is not a Huawei insider but a journalist who compiled accounts from former Huawei employees and public sources.

**Content:** A journalist's account of Huawei's management practices, including critical perspectives not present in Tian Tao's work. Contains first-person accounts from former employees describing the public ranking boards (曝光台), transfer order experiences, and the intensity of the campaign model. The book is notable for including employee perspectives on the costs of Huawei's execution system.

**Evidentiary value:** Moderate. First-person employee accounts provide a perspective absent from the official histories. However, the accounts are anonymized and cannot be independently verified. The book is less systematically documented than Tian Tao's work.

**Limitations:** Relies on former employee testimony rather than internal documents. The accounts are anecdotal, not systematic. The book's critical perspective is a useful counterweight to the official narrative but does not constitute independent verification.

---

### 4. *Huawei Executive Speeches* (《华为高管讲话》)
**Internal publication, multiple volumes, 2001-2018.**

**Author status:** Compiled by Huawei's internal communications department. Not commercially published but circulated widely enough to be cited in multiple books about Huawei.

**Content:** Collected speeches and internal documents by Ren Zhengfei and other Huawei executives. Volume 1 (2001) contains the "wear American shoes" (穿美国鞋) speech. Volume 2 (2010) contains "Let Those Who Hear the Cannon Fire Make Decisions" (让听得见炮声的人做决策) and "The Grayscale of Leadership" (领导的灰度). Volume 3 (2015) contains the "Big Platform" (大平台) speech and the "Squad-Leader Warfare" (班长战争) document.

**Evidentiary value:** Very high for Ren Zhengfei's documented statements. These are primary source documents—the original speeches and essays that are quoted in secondary sources.

**Limitations:** Internal publication; not independently verified against original recordings. The compilation process may have edited or omitted material. Access is limited; the volumes are not commercially available but are cited extensively in Tian Tao's and De Cremer's books.

---

### 5. Huang Weiwei (黄卫伟) et al., *Huawei's Basic Law* (《华为基本法》)
**Internal publication, 1998. Published in various forms since.**

**Author status:** Huang Weiwei is a professor at Renmin University and led the academic team that helped Huawei draft its "Basic Law" (基本法)—a foundational document articulating Huawei's values, principles, and management philosophy.

**Content:** The "Basic Law" is not a legal document but a statement of organizational principles drafted between 1996 and 1998 with input from Ren Zhengfei and external academics. It covers Huawei's commitment to R&D investment, the "customer-first" principle, the rejection of family management, and the foundations of the employee shareholding system.

**Evidentiary value:** High as a historical document. The Basic Law is the foundational text for Huawei's management system.

**Limitations:** Aspirational, not descriptive. The Basic Law states what Huawei aimed to be, not necessarily what it was at any given moment. Its significance is as a founding document of Huawei's management philosophy.

## ALIBABA (阿里巴巴)

### 6. Zeng Ming (曾鸣), *Smart Business* (《智能商业》)
**CITIC Press (中信出版社), 2018. Chinese original of the English edition published by HBR Press.**

**Author status:** Alibaba's Chief Strategy Officer (2006-2019) and primary architect of the Middle Platform strategy. Professor at Cheung Kong Graduate School of Business before and after Alibaba.

**Content:** The Chinese original of *Smart Business*. Covers the Middle Platform architecture, the strategic investment pool, the Freshippo launch, the 2015 Singles' Day payment crisis, and the platform coordination model. The Chinese edition may contain detail not included in the English translation.

**Evidentiary value:** Very high. First-person account from the chief strategy officer. The Chinese original is the primary text; the English edition is a translation.

**Limitations:** Company insider account. Single-source. Presents Alibaba's decisions favorably.

---

### 7. *Alibaba Internal Speeches* (《阿里巴巴内部讲话》)
**Internal publication, multiple volumes, approximately 2010-2015.**

**Author status:** Compiled by Alibaba's internal communications department. Contains speeches by Jack Ma, Daniel Zhang, and other Alibaba executives.

**Content:** Jack Ma's 2009 speech on "trial and error" (试错) and the Alibaba Cloud investment. Daniel Zhang's 2015 internal letter on the Middle Platform strategy. Jack Ma's 2011 email diagnosing "big company disease" (大公司病). Jack Ma's 2013 email announcing the 25-business unit restructuring.

**Evidentiary value:** High for executive statements. Primary source documents.

**Limitations:** Internal publication; not commercially available. Cited in secondary sources (Zeng Ming, Edward Tse). The compilation process may have edited material.

### 8. Li Xiang (李翔), *Alibaba: The Rise of an E-commerce Giant* (《阿里巴巴：电商帝国的崛起》)
**Zhejiang University Press (浙江大学出版社), 2015. Approximately 320 pages.**

**Author status:** Independent business journalist and author.

**Content:** A journalistic account of Alibaba's growth from 1999 to 2014. Covers the early entrepreneurial phase, the Taobao vs. eBay China competition, the Alipay separation, and the 2013 restructuring. Less management detail than Zeng Ming's book but provides external perspective and critical distance.

**Evidentiary value:** Moderate. Journalistic account based on public sources and interviews. Provides a counterbalance to the insider narrative.

**Limitations:** Less operational detail than Zeng Ming. Does not cover post-2014 developments including the Middle Platform strategy. Journalistic rather than documentary.

## TENCENT (腾讯)

### 9. Wu Xiaobo (吴晓波), *Tencent: The Story of a Chinese Internet Giant* (《腾讯传》)
**CITIC Press (中信出版社), 2017. Approximately 400 pages.**

**Author status:** Independent business journalist and author. Wu Xiaobo is one of China's most respected business writers, known for *Thirty Years of Turbulence* (《激荡三十年》), a widely cited history of Chinese business reform. The Tencent book was written with Tencent's cooperation and based on extensive interviews with Pony Ma, Allen Zhang, and other executives.

**Content:** The most comprehensive account of Tencent's history and management practices. Chapter 8 documents the WeChat horse race (2010-2011) in detail: the three competing teams, Allen Zhang's Guangzhou team's victory, Pony Ma's personal funding approval, and the fate of the losing teams. Chapter 10 covers Tencent's "platform plus ecosystem" model, internal headhunting practices, and Pony Ma's "connectors vs. connected" distinction.

**Evidentiary value:** High for the WeChat development story and Tencent's strategic evolution. Wu Xiaobo's journalistic reputation and named sources (Pony Ma, Allen Zhang) give the account credibility.

**Limitations:** Company-cooperative history. Wu Xiaobo is independent but respectful. The book is a business history, not a management analysis. Operational detail on governance mechanisms is less granular than the Huawei or Alibaba sources.

### 10. Chen Xuejun (陈学军), *Tencent's Management Philosophy* (《腾讯管理法》)
**China Machine Press (机械工业出版社), 2018. Approximately 300 pages.**

**Author status:** Management writer and researcher. Not a Tencent insider.

**Content:** An analytical account of Tencent's management practices, including the product development philosophy ("small steps, fast running" / 小步快跑), the horse racing mechanism (赛马机制), and the relationship between Tencent's platform and its ecosystem partners.

**Evidentiary value:** Moderate. Analytical rather than documentary. Synthesizes public information and interviews. Provides a structured overview of Tencent's management approach.

**Limitations:** Secondary source. Lacks the primary access of Wu Xiaobo's book. More analytical framework than documentary evidence.

## HAIER (海尔)

### 11. Zhang Ruimin (张瑞敏), *The Haier Model* (《海尔模式》)
**CITIC Press (中信出版社), 2018. Approximately 350 pages.**

**Author status:** Founder, former CEO and Chairman of Haier Group. Creator of Rendanheyi.

**Content:** Zhang Ruimin's own account of the Rendanheyi model: its origins, philosophy, implementation, and evolution. Covers the ZZJYT phase (2005-2012), the Xiaowei restructuring (2013-2015), and the "ecosystem brand" phase (2019 onward). The book is the most comprehensive statement of Rendanheyi by its creator.

**Evidentiary value:** Very high for the model's design intent and Zhang's stated rationale. The primary source on Rendanheyi.

**Limitations:** Founder's account. Presents the model in its best light. Aspirations and achievements are not clearly distinguished. No independent verification of performance claims.

---

### 12. Cao Yang (曹阳), *The Haier Transformation* (《海尔转型》)
**CITIC Press (中信出版社), 2016. Approximately 280 pages.**

**Author status:** Business journalist and author. Not a Haier insider but granted access for this book.

**Content:** A journalistic account of Haier's transformation from a traditional manufacturer to a platform organization. Covers the ZZJYT restructuring, the early Xiaowei implementation, and the challenges of the transition. More critical distance than Zhang Ruimin's own account.

**Evidentiary value:** Moderate. Journalistic account with company access. Provides external perspective on the transformation's difficulties.

**Limitations:** Published in 2016; does not cover the full abolition of middle management or the ecosystem brand phase. Journalistic rather than documentary.

## BYD (比亚迪)

### 13. Wang Chuanfu (王传福) [attributed], *The BYD Way* (《比亚迪之道》)
**China Machine Press (机械工业出版社), 2017. Approximately 320 pages.**

**Author status:** Attributed to Wang Chuanfu with co-authors. Wang is the founder and CEO of BYD. The extent of his direct authorship versus co-author contribution is unclear.

**Content:** BYD's vertically integrated manufacturing model, the "manual + automation" (人工+自动化) semi-automated production philosophy, and the company's strategic pivot from batteries to automobiles to electric vehicles. Documents BYD's approach to cost control through vertical integration (insourcing components that competitors outsource).

**Evidentiary value:** Moderate. Provides the founder's stated philosophy and BYD's strategic rationale. However, the book's authorship attribution is ambiguous, and it reads more as a company-approved profile than a founder's memoir.

**Limitations:** Ambiguous authorship. Company-approved. Less operational detail than the Huawei or Haier sources. BYD's management practices are less thoroughly documented in accessible sources than the internet companies in this bibliography.

**China Economic Press (中国经济出版社), 2022. Approximately 280 pages.**

## XIAOMI (小米)

### 15. Lei Jun (雷军), *Xiaomi: The Inside Story* (《小米：从零到千亿的秘密》)
**CITIC Press (中信出版社), 2018. Approximately 320 pages.**

**Author status:** Lei Jun is the founder and CEO of Xiaomi. The book was published around Xiaomi's Hong Kong IPO. Co-authors contributed to the writing.

**Content:** Xiaomi's founding philosophy, the "iron triangle" (铁三角) of product, price, and efficiency, the ecosystem company model (300+ partner companies), and the MIJIA IoT platform. Lei Jun articulates the rationale for minority equity stakes in ecosystem partners rather than full ownership: "If we own them, they become employees. Employees don't innovate like entrepreneurs."

**Evidentiary value:** High for Lei Jun's stated strategy and Xiaomi's business model. The primary source for the ecosystem company model's design intent.

**Limitations:** CEO autobiography published around IPO. Presents Xiaomi in a favorable light. Less operational detail on internal management practices than the Alibaba or Huawei sources. Marketing-oriented in places.

### 16. Li Zhiyong (李志勇), *Xiaomi's Ecosystem: How 300 Companies Were Built* (《小米生态链：300家公司的崛起》)
**China Machine Press (机械工业出版社), 2019. Approximately 300 pages.**

**Author status:** Technology journalist covering China's consumer electronics and IoT sectors.

**Content:** A detailed account of Xiaomi's ecosystem company model: how Xiaomi identifies, invests in, and coordinates 300+ hardware startups. Documents the design standards, quality requirements, MIJIA platform integration, and commercial incentives that Xiaomi uses to coordinate partners without owning them.

**Evidentiary value:** Moderate to high. The most detailed available account of Xiaomi's ecosystem coordination mechanisms. Journalistic but well-sourced.

**Limitations:** Secondary source. Relies on interviews and public information rather than internal documents. Xiaomi's internal management practices are less documented than its ecosystem model.

## BYTEDANCE (字节跳动)

### 17. Chen Lin (陈林), *ByteDance: From Zero to One* (《字节跳动：从0到1的秘密》)
**CITIC Press (中信出版社), 2020. Approximately 320 pages.**

**Author status:** Technology journalist. Not a ByteDance employee. The book is based on internal ByteDance sources and interviews but is not officially sanctioned.

**Content:** The most detailed available account of ByteDance's internal practices. Documents Zhang Yiming's founding document (2012) and the "Context, Not Control" (上下文，而非控制) principle. Chapter 3 covers the recommendation engine as shared platform and the "Platform First" development principle. Chapter 4 covers OKR transparency, internal talent mobility, the "No-Meeting Wednesday" (无会星期三) policy, the Douyin content moderation escalation (9 hours, asynchronous), and the education technology horse race (2018-2020).

**Evidentiary value:** Moderate. The most detailed account available for ByteDance, a company that is less documented than Huawei or Alibaba. Not officially sanctioned, which may increase independence but reduces access to formal documentation.

**Limitations:** Single-source account. Cannot be verified against ByteDance internal documents. ByteDance is younger and less documented than the other companies in this bibliography. The book's sources are anonymous.

## Critical Assessment of the Source Base

**The core problem:** The most detailed, operationally specific sources on Chinese corporate management are company-cooperative or company-authored. Tian Tao (Huawei), Zeng Ming (Alibaba), Zhang Ruimin (Haier), and Lei Jun (Xiaomi) are either company insiders or semi-official historians writing with company approval. This does not mean their accounts are false, but it means they are curated. Independent verification through external sources (Caixin, LatePost, Wu Xiaobo) is available for specific incidents but not for systematic assessment of management practices.

**The independent check:** Wu Xiaobo's Tencent book and Chen Lin's ByteDance book represent a middle ground—written by independent journalists with access but not company control. Caixin and LatePost provide the most independent reporting, but in article format rather than comprehensive book-length treatment.

**The missing sources:** There is no independent, book-length, investigative account of Huawei's internal management practices comparable to the independent journalism on Tencent and ByteDance. There is no book-length treatment of Midea's T+3 transformation beyond the Duke GVC Center case study. Ping An's internal management practices are documented in annual reports and investor presentations but not in a comprehensive book. JD.com's management practices are documented only in Liu Qiangdong's autobiography, a CEO account.

**The honest assessment:** The Chinese-language source base is richer than the English-language base but suffers from the same fundamental limitation: the sources with the most access have the least independence, and the sources with the most independence have the least access. The convergence of company accounts (Tian Tao and De Cremer et al. on Huawei, for example) provides some corroboration, but independent systematic verification of management practice claims is not available in any language.
