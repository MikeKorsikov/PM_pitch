## The Recurring System: The "Campaign-Platform Duality"

The strongest documented cases reveal a system built on a structural tension between two operating modes: a **Platform Mode** that provides stability, reuse, and efficiency, and a **Campaign Mode** that provides speed, focus, and breakthrough. The system's effectiveness comes not from either mode alone but from the documented mechanisms that govern the transition between them.

This is not a metaphor. Each component of this system is documented with named mechanisms, specific company practices, and in many cases the original Chinese terminology.

---

## Component 1: Strategic Direction — The Bifurcated Investment Architecture

### Documented Mechanism: Dual Funding Structures

The strongest cases share a common approach to strategic direction: they operate two funding mechanisms in parallel, separating steady-state resource allocation from strategic initiative funding.

**Huawei:** Annual budget process for ongoing operations; "Strategic Reserves" (战略储备) controlled personally by Ren Zhengfei for campaigns, deployable within 48 hours. (Tian Tao, *The Huawei Story*, Chapter 10, pp. 245-250; De Cremer et al., 2017, pp. 190-195)

**Alibaba:** Annual budget for business units; "Strategic Investment Pool" controlled by the CEO (Jack Ma, then Daniel Zhang), deployable within weeks, used for initiatives like Freshippo. (Zeng Ming, *Smart Business*, Chapter 6, pp. 165-172)

**JD.com:** Annual departmental budgets; "Campaign Fund" controlled by Liu Qiangdong personally, deployable immediately during declared campaigns. (Liu Qiangdong, *The JD.com Story*, Chapter 6, pp. 140-145)

**Ping An:** Annual budget for insurance, banking, and asset management operations; "Innovation and Technology Fund" of RMB 7.2 billion (2017), overseen by Chairman and Group CFO, disclosed in audited annual reports. (Ping An 2017 Annual Report, pp. 15-18)

### The Pattern

All four companies maintain a dual funding structure: annual budget for the known, CEO-controlled strategic pool for the unknown. This is the mechanism that separates the speed of strategic response from the annual budget cycle. A strategic opportunity identified in March does not wait until the next budget cycle to be funded.

### How It Connects to the Other Domains

The strategic pool funds campaigns. The annual budget funds the platform. This funding architecture is the financial expression of the campaign-platform duality.

## Component 2: Organisational Structure — The Platform + Frontline Duality

### Documented Mechanism: Shared Platform Layer + Autonomous Frontline Units

The strongest cases have all invested in a shared platform layer that provides common capabilities, while simultaneously creating autonomous frontline units that consume those capabilities.

**Alibaba:** The Middle Platform (中台) provides shared services (user identity, payments, logistics, data, AI). Frontline business units (Freshippo, Taobao, Tmall) consume these services and focus on differentiation. Zeng Ming documents that 80% of a new business unit's required capabilities come from the platform. (Zeng Ming, *Smart Business*, Chapter 5, pp. 125-158)

**Huawei:** Shared hardware modules and software libraries provide common engineering capabilities. Product teams are required to use them (70%+ reuse target) and are evaluated on reuse at IPD phase gates. Product teams focus on product-specific differentiation. (Tian Tao, Chapter 7, pp. 165-180; De Cremer et al., pp. 175-185)

**Haier:** The Haier platform provides manufacturing, supply chain, finance, and HR services. Xiaowei (micro-enterprises, 4,000+) consume these services but can also source externally if the platform is not competitive. Xiaowei focus entirely on customer needs. (Zhang Ruimin, HBR 2016; Fischer et al., *Reinventing Giants*, 2013, Chapter 7)

**ByteDance:** The recommendation engine and AI infrastructure serve as a shared platform. Product teams (Douyin, Toutiao, Xigua) build on this platform and focus on content strategy, user experience, and community building. (Chen Lin, *ByteDance*, 2020, Chapters 3-4)

### The Pattern

All four companies have created a structural separation between the platform layer (shared, standardized, insourced) and the frontline layer (autonomous, differentiated, customer-facing). This is not a matrix organisation. It is a platform + portfolio structure.

### How It Connects to the Other Domains

The platform layer is the architectural foundation that makes campaigns affordable. When 80% of required capabilities already exist as platform services, the marginal cost of launching a campaign—or launching multiple competing teams in a horse race—drops dramatically. The platform is what makes internal competition economically rational.

## Component 3: Technology Architecture — Platform-Embedded Governance

### Documented Mechanism: Governance by Platform, Not by Committee

The strongest cases have embedded governance into their technology architecture rather than layering it on top as a separate process.

**Alibaba:** Three rules documented by Zeng Ming (*Smart Business*, Chapter 5, pp. 140-145): (1) Permission to use platform services is automatic—no approval required. (2) Modification of platform services is forbidden—frontline teams have consume-only access. (3) Duplication is not allowed—teams cannot build their own version of an existing platform service. Architecture review is triggered only when a team wants to build something new, not when they want to use what exists.

**Huawei:** "Compliance by construction" (De Cremer et al., pp. 210-215): Product teams build with approved shared modules. If they use the approved modules, their product is compliant without needing architecture review. Review happens at the module level (when the platform team builds or modifies a module), not at the product level.

**ByteDance:** "Platform First" principle enforced through code review (Chen Lin, Chapter 3, pp. 80-85): if an engineer builds something that duplicates an existing platform service, the code will not be merged. The default is platform. The exception is bespoke, and the exception requires explicit justification.

### The Pattern

Governance is not a committee that meets monthly. Governance is the platform itself—its APIs, its service catalog, its code review, its automated enforcement. Using what exists requires zero governance. Building something new triggers review. This is the architectural mechanism that prevents the platform from becoming a bottleneck while still preventing fragmentation.

### How It Connects to the Other Domains

Platform-embedded governance is what enables frontline units to move fast without creating chaos. They don't need permission to use platform services. They can't break the platform by modifying it. They can't fragment it by duplicating it. The governance is automatic, not bureaucratic. This is the technology mechanism that enables the organisational structure (autonomous frontline + shared platform) to function at speed.

## Component 4: Project Governance — The Campaign as Temporary Override

### Documented Mechanism: Campaign Governance with Extraordinary Authority

The strongest cases share a common approach to governing strategic initiatives: they declare a "campaign" (战役) that temporarily overrides normal governance and concentrates authority in a single commander.

**Huawei:** The campaign commander (总指挥) is issued formal resource requisition authority (资源调配权) and transfer order authority (调令), enabling them to requisition resources and personnel across departmental boundaries without negotiation. The commander's authority is temporary (campaign duration only) and reverts after the campaign. (Tian Tao, Chapter 10, pp. 242-248; De Cremer et al., pp. 185-200)

**Alibaba:** During campaigns (e.g., Singles' Day, new business launches), a "fast track" governance process replaces standard multi-stage approval. The sponsor can invoke "campaign governance" (战役治理) with a single approval and post-hoc compliance review. During the 2015 Singles' Day payment crisis, Daniel Zhang made a decision involving substantial unbudgeted expenditure in 10 minutes—a decision that would normally take two weeks. (Zeng Ming, Chapter 6, pp. 162-165)

**JD.com:** During campaigns (e.g., 6.18 shopping festival), campaign commanders have spending authority from Liu Qiangdong's campaign fund without prior finance approval. Post-campaign accounting reconciles actual spend. Liu personally commanded the 6.18 war room and resolved a warehouse saturation crisis in approximately 4 hours, including a personal CEO-to-CEO call with a logistics partner. (Liu Qiangdong, Chapters 6-7)

### The Pattern

A campaign is a temporary governance override. Normal rules are not broken but compressed. The commander has extraordinary authority for a defined period with a defined objective. After the campaign, authority reverts. Post-hoc accountability replaces pre-approval.

### How It Connects to the Other Domains

The campaign is the governance mechanism that connects strategic direction to execution. The strategic pool funds the campaign. The platform provides its capabilities. The commander has the authority to mobilise both. The campaign is the mechanism that converts strategic intent into concentrated organisational action.

## Component 5: Resource Allocation — The Talent Mobility Architecture

### Documented Mechanism: Structural Rules That Prevent Talent Hoarding

The strongest cases have created structural rules that enable talent to flow to strategic campaigns without being blocked by current managers.

**Huawei:** The transfer order (调令) system (Tian Tao, Chapter 10, pp. 242-248; De Cremer et al., pp. 195-200): Campaign commanders can issue transfer orders to any employee within the relevant technical domain. The employee's home department must release them within 72 hours. The department head cannot refuse; they can appeal after compliance. The employee is contractually required to accept. This is a structural override of normal managerial authority over talent.

**Alibaba:** The "borrow-and-lend" (借调) system (Zeng Ming, Chapter 6, pp. 165-168): Initiative leaders can negotiate to borrow talent from other business units. If the lending unit refuses, the initiative leader escalates to the CEO, whose decision is final. An internal talent market allows employees to apply directly to strategic initiatives.

**ByteDance:** The internal transfer rule (Chen Lin, Chapter 4, pp. 90-95): Employees can apply for any internal position without informing their current manager. If they receive an offer, the current manager cannot block the transfer. The manager is notified only after the offer is accepted. This is explicitly designed to prevent talent hoarding.

**Ping An:** The Talent Mobility Program (Ping An 2019 Annual Report, pp. 42-45; 2019 Sustainability Report): All internal positions posted on internal platform. Employees with 12+ months in role can apply. Current manager notified but cannot block. 15,000+ employees moved in 2019 (~4% of workforce). Strategic initiatives receive "priority staffing" status.

### The Pattern

In every documented case, there is a structural rule that prevents managers from blocking talent movement to strategic priorities. The rule varies (command-based at Huawei, market-based at ByteDance, hybrid at Alibaba and Ping An), but the principle is consistent: the organisation's need to concentrate talent on strategic priorities overrides the individual manager's desire to retain talent.

### How It Connects to the Other Domains

Talent mobility is the human resource allocation mechanism that connects campaigns to the platform. Campaigns need the best people. The anti-hoarding rules ensure they can get them. Without these rules, the campaign commander's formal authority would be undermined by managers protecting their talent. The anti-hoarding rules make the campaign model operationally viable.

## Component 6: The Feedback Loop — Closed-Loop Accountability

### Documented Mechanism: Daily Rhythm with Personal Consequence

The strongest cases operate a feedback loop that is faster and more personally consequential than Western governance norms.

**Ping An:** The "Morning Market" (早市) (Ping An 2013 Annual Report, Chairman's Statement, pp. 4-5; *Caixin*, 2016): Daily 8:00 AM review of key metrics. Any deviation assigned an owner and corrective action. Corrective action must be initiated by close of business. Next morning: reviewed. If gap persists, escalated to Chairman Ma Mingzhe personally. Claims processing backlog (2015) escalated from detection to CEO approval in approximately 30 hours.

**Huawei:** Escalation protocol with mandated response times (De Cremer et al., pp. 201-205): Level 1 (team lead): 2 hours to acknowledge, 24 hours to resolve or escalate. Level 2 (department head): 4 hours to acknowledge, 48 hours to resolve or escalate. Levels 3, 4, and 5 with successively longer windows. If any level fails to respond, automatic escalation to the next level. Non-responding manager recorded as having caused the delay.

**JD.com:** The public pledge (军令状) with video playback (Liu Qiangdong, Chapter 6, pp. 145-148): Regional directors publicly commit to targets on video. Quarterly review meetings play back the video. Director who failed must watch their own pledge before explaining the shortfall. This makes the memory of failure inescapable.

### The Pattern

Feedback loops operate on a daily or event-driven rhythm, not a monthly steering committee rhythm. Accountability is personal and visible, not organisational and private. The "closed loop" (闭环) must be closed within a defined time window; an open loop is a failure of responsibility.

### How It Connects to the Other Domains

The closed-loop accountability system is the feedback mechanism that connects execution back to strategy. Campaigns are time-boxed. Resources are allocated. Talent is mobilised. The closed loop ensures that deviation from plan is detected and corrected within hours or days, not weeks or months. This is the mechanism that enables the system to operate at campaign speed without losing strategic coherence.

## The Complete System: How the Six Components Interconnect

The recurring system documented across the strongest cases connects the five domains you specified in the following way. The interconnections are my analytical inference, grounded in the documented mechanisms described above.

**Strategic Direction** sets the priorities through a dual funding architecture: annual budget for the platform, strategic pool for campaigns. This is the financial expression of the platform-campaign duality.

**Organisational Structure** provides the structural expression of the same duality: a shared platform layer that provides common capabilities, and autonomous frontline units that consume them. The platform reduces the marginal cost of new initiatives, making campaigns and horse races affordable.

**Technology Architecture** embeds governance into the platform itself. Frontline units consume platform services without permission. They cannot modify or duplicate them. Governance is automatic, not bureaucratic. This prevents the platform from becoming a bottleneck while preventing fragmentation.

**Project Governance** provides the temporary override mechanism: the campaign. When a strategic initiative is declared, a commander receives extraordinary authority for a defined period with a defined objective. Normal governance is compressed, not broken. Post-hoc accountability replaces pre-approval.

**Resource Allocation** provides the talent mobility architecture: structural rules that prevent managers from hoarding talent. Campaign commanders can mobilise the people they need. The organisation's strategic priorities override individual managers' talent retention.

**The Feedback Loop** provides the closed-loop accountability that connects execution back to strategy. Deviation is detected within hours, escalated within a defined timeframe, and corrected. The loop is closed. Accountability is personal.

**The system works as a whole because each component reinforces the others.** The strategic pool funds campaigns. The platform makes campaigns affordable. Embedded governance prevents the platform from becoming a bottleneck. Campaign authority overrides normal organisational friction. Anti-hoarding rules ensure talent flows to campaigns. Closed-loop accountability ensures deviation is corrected at campaign speed.

Remove any component and the system degrades:
- Without the strategic pool, campaigns wait for the annual budget cycle.
- Without the platform, campaigns are too expensive to run as a routine mechanism.
- Without embedded governance, the platform becomes a bottleneck or fragments.
- Without campaign authority, normal organisational friction slows strategic initiatives.
- Without anti-hoarding rules, talent stays in business-as-usual roles.
- Without closed-loop accountability, deviation accumulates until the next steering committee.
