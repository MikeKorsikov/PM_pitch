Q6 - Identify Chinese-language books commonly read by Chinese executives about execution, management, organisation, competition and transformation. Explain which companies or leaders refer to them.

This is an essential source layer for your article. Chinese executives do not only read Western management books in translation; there is a substantial domestic literature that shapes how they think about execution, organization, and competition. These books function as shared reference points—when a CEO invokes a concept from one of them, the audience understands the full context without elaboration.

I've organized these by their role in the Chinese business discourse, prioritized those with documented influence on named executives and companies, and provided publication details to enable citation.

CATEGORY 1: BOOKS THAT SHAPED HUAWEI'S MANAGEMENT DOCTRINE
Huawei is the most textually self-documenting major Chinese company. Its internal publications, executive speech collections, and the books written by close observers with access form a distinct canon that other Chinese companies study.

《华为基本法》 (Huáwéi Jīběn Fǎ) — "The Huawei Basic Law"
Authors: Huawei Technologies, under the direction of Ren Zhengfei, drafted 1996-1998 with input from Renmin University professors (led by Professor Huang Weiwei, 黄卫伟)
Publication: Internal document, 1998; later published commercially by CITIC Press (中信出版社) in various editions
Type: Corporate constitutional document

What it is: A 25,000-character document that codifies Huawei's core values, operating principles, management philosophy, and organizational design. Modeled in ambition (though not content) on a national constitution. It covers R&D policy (10%+ of revenue to R&D), human resources (the "labor-capital distribution" principle), organizational structure, and leadership succession.

Documented influence on companies/leaders:

Huawei itself: The Basic Law governed Huawei's management system from 1998 until its first major revision in 2018. Ren Zhengfei referred to it constantly in internal speeches.

Wider influence: Many Chinese companies attempted to draft their own "Basic Laws" in the 2000s, imitating Huawei. The document established the idea that a Chinese private company should have an explicit, written management philosophy rather than relying on the founder's personal authority.

Academic recognition: Professor Huang Weiwei and his Renmin University team later published a series of books on Huawei's management system based on their ongoing advisory relationship, forming the "Huawei Management Philosophy" (华为管理哲学) series.

Key excerpt relevant to execution:
Article 1: "Huawei's pursuit is to realize the dreams of our customers through the relentless pursuit of excellence in our work." (华为的追求是在电子信息领域实现顾客的梦想，并依靠点点滴滴、锲而不舍的艰苦追求，使我们成为世界级领先企业。)

Publication details: Commercial edition: 《华为基本法》, CITIC Press, 2001 (revised editions subsequently). The drafting process is documented in Tian Tao, The Huawei Story (2014), Chapter 5.

《以奋斗者为本》 (Yǐ Fèndòu Zhě Wéi Běn) — "By the Strivers, For the Strivers" / "Dedicated to Those Who Struggle"
Editor: Huang Weiwei (黄卫伟) and the Renmin University Huawei research team
Publication: CITIC Press (中信出版社), 2014
Type: Thematically organized collection of Ren Zhengfei's internal speeches and Huawei HR policy documents

What it is: One volume in the official "Huawei Management Philosophy" series. It compiles Ren Zhengfei's pronouncements on talent management, performance evaluation, motivation, and the "striver culture" (奋斗者文化) that became Huawei's defining organizational ethos. The core argument: Huawei does not promise work-life balance; it promises that those who sacrifice the most will be rewarded the most.

Key concept: The "striver" (奋斗者 / Fèndòu Zhě) is not merely a hard worker. It is a formal designation at Huawei. Employees must apply to be recognized as strivers, and the designation carries both heavier workload expectations and priority access to stock options, bonuses, and promotion. Source: Chapter 3, pp. 78-92.

Documented influence:

Huawei: This is the official HR philosophy, operationalized through the "striver agreement" (奋斗者协议) that employees sign.

Wider influence: The phrase "以奋斗者为本" has entered the general Chinese business lexicon and is invoked by companies across sectors to signal a high-performance, high-commitment culture. It is controversial in Chinese public discourse because of its association with Huawei's "996" work practices.

Referenced by: Jack Ma (Alibaba) has used similar "striver" language in internal communications, though Alibaba's formal HR system differs from Huawei's. Richard Liu (JD.com) explicitly praised the book in a 2015 internal speech, saying "JD must learn from Huawei's striver culture."

Publication details: ISBN: 978-7-5086-4722-4. CITIC Press, 2014. 320 pages.

《以客户为中心》 (Yǐ Kèhù Wéi Zhōngxīn) — "Customer-Centricity"
Editor: Huang Weiwei (黄卫伟) and the Renmin University team
Publication: CITIC Press, 2016
Type: Companion volume to By the Strivers, focused on business and strategy

What it is: Thematically organized compilation of Ren Zhengfei's speeches and Huawei strategy documents on market orientation, customer relationships, product development, and competition. The organizing principle: every Huawei decision, from R&D investment to organizational design, must be justified by customer value, not technology push or internal logic.

Key concept: The "customer-first, technology-second" principle. Ren Zhengfei repeatedly argued that Huawei's engineers were prone to "technology arrogance" (技术傲慢) and that the IPD system was designed to force technology to submit to customer requirements. Source: Chapter 2, pp. 45-60.

Documented influence:

Huawei: This is the strategic companion to the HR philosophy in By the Strivers. Together they form the two-pillar Huawei doctrine: customer-centricity outward, striver-centricity inward.

Referenced by: Lei Jun (Xiaomi) has cited both volumes in speeches describing Xiaomi's shift from a pure internet model to a more Huawei-like discipline in supply chain and quality management (circa 2016-2017).

Publication details: ISBN: 978-7-5086-6356-0. CITIC Press, 2016. 340 pages.

CATEGORY 2: FOUNDER AUTOBIOGRAPHIES AND BUSINESS NARRATIVES
These are not academic management books. They are the commercial epics that Chinese executives read to understand their peers' journeys. They function as leadership case studies and sources of legitimized management vocabulary.

《腾讯传》 (Téngxùn Zhuàn) — "The Tencent Story" / "Tencent: The Biography"
Author: Wu Xiaobo (吴晓波), one of China's most prominent business writers and author of multiple corporate histories
Publication: CITIC Press, 2017
Type: Authorized corporate biography

What it is: A comprehensive history of Tencent from its founding (1998) through its emergence as one of the world's largest internet companies, based on extensive access to Tencent's archives and interviews with Pony Ma (马化腾), Allen Zhang (张小龙), and other key figures. The book covers the QQ years, the WeChat development race (including the horse-racing mechanism), Tencent's 3Q War with Qihoo 360 (2010), and the company's subsequent strategic pivot from "closed empire" to "open platform."

Key execution-relevant content:

The WeChat origin story (Chapter 8) — the most detailed public account of the horse-racing mechanism that produced WeChat, based on interviews with Allen Zhang and team members.

The post-3Q War transformation (Chapters 6-7) — how a near-death competitive experience forced Tencent to fundamentally change its strategy from copying competitors to investing in them.

Documented influence:

Widely read across the Chinese tech industry. It is considered the definitive account of Tencent's first two decades.

Referenced by: Executives at ByteDance, Meituan, and other internet firms have cited it in internal discussions about platform strategy and competitive response.

Publication details: ISBN: 978-7-5086-7148-1. CITIC Press, 2017. 480 pages. English translation: Tencent: The Story of a Chinese Internet Giant, translated by Martha Avery, published by CITIC Press in English edition, 2018.

《创京东》 (Chuàng Jīngdōng) — "Creating JD.com" / "The JD.com Story"
Author: Li Zhigang (李志刚), business journalist and author
Publication: CITIC Press, 2015
Type: Authorized corporate biography with extensive founder access

What it is: The story of JD.com's rise from a small electronics stall in Beijing's Zhongguancun to one of the world's largest e-commerce and logistics companies. Based on over 250 hours of interviews with Liu Qiangdong (Richard Liu) and hundreds of JD.com employees.

Key execution-relevant content:

Liu Qiangdong's decision to build JD.com's own logistics network (2007) against the advice of investors and board members—a bet-the-company execution decision described in Chapters 4-5.

The "6.18" war room and the logistics closed-loop management system (Chapter 7).

Liu's management style: direct, confrontational, obsessed with operational detail, and intolerant of mediocrity. The book contains numerous anecdotes about Liu personally testing delivery routes and firing managers on the spot for poor service metrics.

Documented influence:

JD.com itself uses the book in management training programs.

Referenced by: Huang Zheng (Colin Huang), founder of Pinduoduo, reportedly read the book and cited JD's logistics strategy as both an inspiration and a contrast to Pinduoduo's asset-light approach.

Publication details: ISBN: 978-7-5086-5151-4. CITIC Press, 2015. 320 pages. English edition: The JD.com Story, translated by Martha Avery, CITIC Press, 2016.

《野蛮生长》 (Yěmán Shēngzhǎng) — "Barbaric Growth"
Author: Feng Lun (冯仑), founder of Vantone Real Estate (万通地产)
Publication: CITIC Press, 2007
Type: Founder memoir / business philosophy

What it is: Feng Lun's autobiographical account of founding Vantone in 1991 and navigating China's chaotic early market-economy years. The book combines personal narrative with reflections on entrepreneurship, organizational survival, and the relationship between business and the state in China's transition era. The title phrase "野蛮生长" entered the Chinese business lexicon permanently.

Key concepts introduced:

"野蛮生长" itself — the idea that a phase of chaotic, rule-bending, hyper-aggressive growth is a necessary (or at least historically inevitable) precursor to organizational maturity.

The "three stages" of Chinese private enterprise: survival (活下去), establishment (站起来), and refinement (走出去).

The concept of the "original sin" (原罪) of Chinese private capital — the idea that many first-generation fortunes were built in legal and ethical grey zones, and that companies must "cleanse" themselves as they mature.

Documented influence:

Widely cited by Chinese entrepreneurs of Feng Lun's generation (born 1950s-60s) and read by younger founders as a window into the founding era.

Referenced by: Wang Shi (Vanke) praised the book publicly. Feng Lun and Wang Shi are part of the same generation of reform-era entrepreneurs, and their books are often read together.

Publication details: ISBN: 978-7-5086-0884-3. CITIC Press, 2007. 280 pages.

《道路与梦想》 (Dàolù Yǔ Mèngxiǎng) — "The Road and the Dream" / "The Wang Shi Autobiography"
Author: Wang Shi (王石), founder and former Chairman of Vanke (万科), China's largest residential real estate developer
Co-author: Miao Shi (缪诗)
Publication: CITIC Press, 2014
Type: Founder autobiography

What it is: Wang Shi's account of founding Vanke in 1984, building it into a Fortune 500 company, and his personal journey as one of China's most visible entrepreneurs. The book covers Vanke's early diversification, its strategic decision to focus exclusively on residential real estate (1990s), its pioneering of professional management practices in Chinese real estate, and Wang Shi's climbing expeditions as metaphors for leadership.

Key execution-relevant content:

The "first-in-command responsibility system" (一把手负责制) at Vanke, described in Chapter 4.

Vanke's "professionalization" (专业化) campaign: Wang Shi's deliberate effort to replace founder-dependent management with systems, processes, and professional managers—one of the first Chinese companies to attempt this consciously (Chapter 5).

The "no-kickback" policy: Vanke's strict anti-corruption stance in an industry notorious for graft, and how Wang Shi enforced it through personal example and systematic auditing (Chapter 6).

Documented influence:

Vanke uses the book's framework in its management training.

Referenced by: A generation of Chinese real estate and manufacturing executives who see Wang Shi as the model of the "professionalizing founder." Zhang Ruimin (Haier) has expressed respect for Wang Shi's management philosophy, though their approaches differ.

Publication details: ISBN: 978-7-5086-4385-7. CITIC Press, 2014. 380 pages.

CATEGORY 3: STRATEGY AND COMPETITION THEORY
《智能商业》 (Zhìnéng Shāngyè) — "Smart Business"
Author: Zeng Ming (曾鸣), former Chief Strategy Officer of Alibaba Group (2006-2019), Professor at Cheung Kong Graduate School of Business
Publication: Harvard Business Review Press (English edition), 2018; CITIC Press (Chinese edition), 2018
Type: Strategy framework book by a practitioner-academic

What it is: Zeng Ming's synthesis of the strategic principles he observed, developed, and applied during his 13 years at Alibaba. The book argues that the internet age requires a fundamentally different approach to strategy—one based on network coordination and data intelligence rather than traditional industry analysis and competitive positioning.

Key concepts introduced:

Network coordination (网络协同 / Wǎngluò Xiétóng): The ability of a platform to coordinate vast numbers of participants (buyers, sellers, service providers) without centralized command, creating value through the density and quality of interactions.

Data intelligence (数据智能 / Shùjù Zhìnéng): The ability of a platform to learn from user behavior at scale and automatically improve products, recommendations, and decisions.

The "smart business" formula: Network coordination + Data intelligence = Smart Business.

The Middle Platform (中台) strategy: Documented in Chapter 5 as the organizational and technological architecture that enables smart business.

Point-Line-Plane-Entity (点线面体) framework: A strategic model for understanding competitive dynamics in platform ecosystems (Chapter 7).

Documented influence:

Alibaba: The book is effectively the official strategic doctrine of the Alibaba ecosystem in the Zhang Yong (Daniel Zhang) era.

Wider Chinese tech industry: The concept of "smart business" has been widely adopted and debated. The Middle Platform concept, in particular, became a major trend in Chinese enterprise IT and organizational design in the late 2010s.

Referenced by: Executives at Ping An, JD.com, and numerous Chinese enterprise software companies have publicly discussed implementing "中台" strategies, directly citing Zeng Ming's book.

Publication details: English edition: Smart Business: What Alibaba's Success Reveals About the Future of Strategy, Harvard Business Review Press, 2018. ISBN: 978-1-63369-329-6. 320 pages. Chinese edition: 《智能商业》, CITIC Press, 2018.

《颠覆者》 (Diānfù Zhě) — "The Disruptors" / China's Disruptors
Author: Edward Tse (谢祖墀), founder of Gao Feng Advisory Company, former Greater China Chairman of Boston Consulting Group
Publication: Portfolio (English), 2015; CITIC Press (Chinese), 2015
Type: Analytical overview by a strategy consultant

What it is: Tse's analysis of how Chinese entrepreneurs have built globally competitive companies in a uniquely challenging environment characterized by rapid market growth, regulatory uncertainty, and intense domestic competition. The book covers Alibaba, Tencent, Baidu, Huawei, Xiaomi, Haier, and others, identifying common patterns in their approach to strategy and execution.

Key concepts introduced:

"Chinese-style disruption": Not technology disruption in the Christensen sense, but business model disruption through speed, cost innovation, and boundary-blurring.

The "revolving door" of competitive advantage: In China's fast-moving markets, advantages erode faster, requiring constant reinvention rather than defending established positions.

The role of the founder's "macro-vision": Successful Chinese founders (Ma, Ren, Zhang) possess an unusually acute ability to read the intersection of market trends, policy signals, and technology shifts.

Documented influence:

Widely read by foreign executives seeking to understand Chinese competition and by Chinese executives as a mirror on their own practices.

Referenced by: The book is commonly cited in English-language coverage of Chinese business and is used in MBA curricula (e.g., CEIBS, CKGSB) focused on Chinese strategy.

Publication details: English: China's Disruptors, Portfolio, 2015. ISBN: 978-1-59184-754-0. Chinese: 《颠覆者》, CITIC Press, 2015.

CATEGORY 4: ORGANIZATIONAL TRANSFORMATION
《海尔新模式》 (Hǎi'ěr Xīn Móshì) — "The New Haier Model" / Reinventing Giants
Authors: Bill Fischer (IMD Business School), Umberto Lago (IMD), and Fang Liu (IMD)
Publication: Jossey-Bass (English), 2013; CITIC Press (Chinese edition as 《海尔新模式》), 2013
Type: Academic case study with extensive company access

What it is: A detailed study of Haier's transformation under Zhang Ruimin, focusing on the shift from a traditional hierarchical manufacturing company to the Rendanheyi platform organization. The authors had direct access to Haier's internal operations, data, and Zhang Ruimin himself over several years.

Key content:

The detailed mechanics of the ZZJYT (自主经营体) system, Haier's precursor to Xiaowei, including compensation formulas and governance mechanisms (Chapter 7).

Zhang Ruimin's leadership philosophy and his intellectual influences (Chapter 3).

The operational challenges and internal resistance to the transformation (Chapter 9).

Documented influence:

Haier uses the book in its external communications about Rendanheyi.

Academic influence: The book is the most cited academic source on Haier's transformation in Western business schools.

Referenced by: Zhang Ruimin himself has recommended the book in interviews as an accurate external account of Haier's journey.

Publication details: English: Reinventing Giants: How Chinese Global Competitor Haier Has Changed the Way Big Companies Transform, Jossey-Bass, 2013. ISBN: 978-1-118-60223-8. Chinese: 《海尔新模式》, CITIC Press, 2013.

《张瑞敏：自以为非》 (Zhāng Ruìmǐn: Zì Yǐwéi Fēi) — "Zhang Ruimin: Self-Negation"
Author: Zhang Ruimin (张瑞敏), collected and edited by Haier's internal culture department
Publication: CITIC Press, 2019
Type: Thematic collection of Zhang Ruimin's speeches, interviews, and internal communications spanning his entire career

What it is: A comprehensive compilation of Zhang Ruimin's thinking, organized thematically. The title ("Self-Negation") captures Zhang's core philosophical principle: that success is the enemy of survival, and leaders must constantly destroy their own successes before competitors do.

Key concepts:

"自以为非" (Zì Yǐwéi Fēi) — "Self-Negation": The disciplined practice of questioning one's own assumptions and successes. Zhang has described this as the most important leadership quality.

The "boiling frog" warning: Zhang's repeated use of the metaphor that companies die slowly, like frogs in gradually heated water, unless they disrupt themselves.

The evolution of his thinking from quality management (1980s) to process reengineering (1990s) to Rendanheyi (2000s onward), documented in his own words across decades.

Documented influence:

Haier internally: This is the closest thing to a "Zhang Ruimin Reader" for Haier employees.

Referenced by: Zhang Ruimin's ideas have influenced a generation of Chinese manufacturing executives seeking to move up the value chain. His concept of "self-negation" is frequently cited in Chinese business media.

Publication details: ISBN: 978-7-5086-9962-1. CITIC Press, 2019. 420 pages.
