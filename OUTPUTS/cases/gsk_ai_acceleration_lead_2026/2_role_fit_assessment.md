## Role Summary
The role is an AI-enabled transformation and delivery role in GSK Development Operations. It focuses on identifying operational bottlenecks in clinical development, shaping AI pilot hypotheses, running pilots with technology teams and vendors, proving value, and scaling adoption where evidence supports it.

This is not primarily a deep ML research or pure AI engineering role. It appears to require a practical combination of AI adoption, workflow analysis, stakeholder management, pilot delivery, value case development, governance, change adoption, and portfolio prioritization.

## Key Requirements Extracted
- Identify AI opportunities by scanning Development Operations workflows.
- Engage clinical operations users and translate bottlenecks into pilot hypotheses.
- Scope and run AI pilots from problem definition to recommendation.
- Use AI-native development tools such as Claude Code and Codex for hands-on use-case testing.
- Partner with enterprise technology teams, vendors, users, and senior stakeholders.
- Build evidence of value using time saved, quality improvement, adoption, risk reduction, and readiness to scale.
- Inform portfolio and investment decisions using pilot findings and operational intelligence.
- Drive workflow redesign, user readiness, change interventions, and scaled adoption.
- Bring pharma strategy, applied AI/technology, transformation, or programme delivery experience.
- Understand clinical operations / clinical development context and GxP expectations.

## Seniority Fit
The role appears aligned-to-stretch. The candidate has senior product, transformation, governance, finance systems, and stakeholder leadership experience. The stretch is domain-specific: clinical operations, clinical development, and pharma AI delivery. The role may be a good fit if positioned as AI-enabled transformation and pilot-to-scale execution rather than deep AI engineering.

## Underqualification Signals
- Direct clinical operations / clinical development experience is a basic qualification and the user confirmed this is not evidenced.
- Pharma AI, GxP, validation, CSV, quality, or regulated technology delivery experience is not evidenced.
- Applied AI delivery in a professional enterprise setting is developing rather than established.
- AI-native rapid prototyping is evidenced through a personal CRM application, not enterprise clinical operations delivery.

## Overqualification Signals
- Candidate may bring broader product ownership, finance systems governance, and transformation leadership than the role requires.
- The application should avoid over-indexing on finance systems and instead translate that experience into AI acceleration, workflow redesign, governance, and value realization.

## Acceptable Gaps
- Deep ML engineering is not clearly required.
- Production-grade ML platform ownership is not clearly required.
- Formal enterprise architecture certification is not required.
- Advanced degree is preferred, not mandatory.
- Pure AI research expertise is not central to the role.
- Personal AI-assisted prototyping can support credibility for AI-native tooling, if described as personal/prototype work.

## Red Flag Gaps
- No direct clinical operations / clinical development experience.
- No pharma AI, GxP, CSV, validation, quality, or regulated AI delivery evidence.
- Claims of direct enterprise AI pilot ownership should not be used.

## Positioning Opportunities
- Position as an AI-enabled transformation and product-governance professional who can turn ambiguous workflow problems into governed pilots and measurable value.
- Emphasize process discovery, bottleneck analysis, roadmap thinking, value realization, stakeholder alignment, vendor coordination, and adoption.
- Translate enterprise/process architecture thinking into pilot-to-scale discipline, shared foundations, reusable patterns, and governance.
- Use the AI-built CRM application as a practical personal prototype example: client, appointment, and finance management built with AI tools including Grok, Cursor, Claude, and GitHub Copilot.
- Frame pharma/regulatory awareness through governance, controlled documentation, risk, validation mindset, and responsible AI.
