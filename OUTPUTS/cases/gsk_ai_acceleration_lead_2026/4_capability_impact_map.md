# Capability Impact Map - GSK AI Acceleration Lead

## Purpose
Map selected pain point hypotheses to business capabilities, technology capabilities, AI acceleration relevance, operating model implications, and transformation opportunities.

## Pain Point to Capability Map
| Pain Point | Business Capabilities | Technology Capabilities | AI Acceleration Relevance | Operating Model Implication | Transformation Opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| slow_change | clinical operations; transformation portfolio governance; product/service portfolio innovation | technology portfolio and value management; software/agile delivery; ITSM/change governance | Helps prioritize pilots that can move quickly from idea to evidence. | Define faster intake, triage, and decision gates. | Create a repeatable pilot-to-scale workflow with clear stop/iterate/scale criteria. |
| low_automation | clinical operations; process management; operational excellence | workflow automation; AI copilots; data/analytics; integration | Targets manual workflow bottlenecks where AI can augment users. | Align users, tech teams, and SMEs around stable process candidates. | Identify high-value automation and AI assistant opportunities. |
| poor_e2e_visibility | portfolio governance; performance management; value realization | reporting; analytics; process intelligence; data platform | Enables evidence-based decisions about which AI pilots create value. | Establish common metrics and learning loops across pilots. | Use adoption, value, quality, and risk signals to inform investment decisions. |
| fragmented_governance | enterprise governance; risk/compliance; vendor management; clinical operations governance | AI governance; identity/access; audit logging; workflow approvals | Keeps AI acceleration safe, consistent, and scalable. | Clarify decision rights across business, AI teams, vendors, and compliance. | Define lightweight responsible-AI governance for pilot delivery and scaling. |
| compliance_audit_complexity | quality/compliance; regulatory governance; controlled documentation | controlled document management; GRC; monitoring/logging; validation evidence | Ensures AI pilots are compliant enough to scale in pharma context. | Embed compliance review, evidence capture, and human validation early. | Reduce late-stage scale blockers through compliance-aware pilot design. |
| knowledge_risk | knowledge management; training/onboarding; adoption management | knowledge assistants; semantic search; document repositories; collaboration platforms | Improves reuse of pilot learning and user guidance. | Assign ownership for reusable learning and adoption assets. | Build reusable patterns, playbooks, and controlled guidance for AI-enabled workflows. |

## Architecture Implications
- AI use cases should be linked to business capabilities, process flows, data needs, controls, and adoption plans.
- Pilot design should consider shared foundations, integration, access, documentation, and scale-readiness from the start.
- Governance should remain lightweight enough for rapid experimentation but strong enough for regulated clinical operations.

## Transformation Opportunities
- Define an AI opportunity intake model based on workflow pain, feasibility, value, risk, and adoption readiness.
- Use process mapping to translate ambiguous bottlenecks into testable AI pilot hypotheses.
- Establish value evidence patterns using time saved, quality improvement, adoption, risk reduction, and readiness to scale.
- Capture reusable learning across pilots to avoid repeating discovery work.
