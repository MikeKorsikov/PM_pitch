# Capability Impact Map - GSK Enterprise Architect

## Purpose
Map validated pain point hypotheses to business capabilities, technology capabilities, and transformation opportunities.

## Pain Point to Capability Map
| Pain Point Hypothesis | Affected Business Capabilities | Affected Technology Capabilities | Enterprise Architecture Relevance | Transformation Opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| slow_change | clinical operations management; transformation portfolio management; change adoption | workflow automation; AI solution lifecycle; delivery governance | Define repeatable pilot-to-scale patterns and decision gates. | Accelerate movement from workflow problem to tested AI capability. |
| poor_e2e_visibility | portfolio prioritization; operational performance management; value realization | data analytics; reporting; process intelligence; KPI management | Connect operational signals, adoption data, and value evidence. | Improve prioritization of AI pilots based on measurable impact. |
| fragmented_governance | enterprise governance; vendor management; risk and compliance management | identity/access control; integration governance; audit logging | Clarify decision rights across business, tech, vendors, and portfolio owners. | Make AI delivery scalable without bypassing compliance or architecture controls. |
| compliance_audit_complexity | quality management; compliance management; clinical development operations | controlled documentation; model risk controls; validation evidence management | Embed compliance, traceability, and auditability into pilot design. | Reduce late-stage compliance friction before scaling AI capabilities. |
| knowledge_risk | knowledge management; training and onboarding; process management | knowledge assistant; document management; semantic search | Capture reusable learning and approved guidance from pilots. | Improve adoption consistency and reduce dependence on individual experts. |

## Architecture Implications
- Use capability mapping to connect AI opportunities to clinical operations outcomes.
- Treat AI pilots as governed products or capabilities, not isolated experiments.
- Define shared foundations, integration boundaries, access controls, and evidence requirements before scale.
- Maintain a clear distinction between public evidence, inferred needs, and application-safe positioning.

## Transformation Opportunities
- Build a repeatable AI pilot intake and prioritization model.
- Use process discovery and workflow analysis to identify high-value bottlenecks.
- Define proof-of-value measures such as time saved, quality improvement, user adoption, risk reduction, and readiness to scale.
- Strengthen governance for AI-enabled clinical operations through human-in-the-loop controls and compliance-ready documentation.
- Reuse learning from pilots through structured knowledge capture and adoption playbooks.
