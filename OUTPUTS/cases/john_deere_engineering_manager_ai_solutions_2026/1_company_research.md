## Dealer Process Landscape

Scope note:
- Excludes generic corporate processes such as HR, legal, tax, and general accounting.
- Public sources are primarily global / US Deere pages. The core process model is likely reusable for Europe, but local finance, warranty, data-consent, distribution, and dealer practices require validation.

### 1. Customer and Opportunity Management
- Identify and qualify farmers, contractors, fleet owners, and other equipment customers.
- Understand operation type, equipment fleet, seasonal needs, capacity constraints, and technology maturity.
- Manage leads, accounts, opportunities, demonstrations, campaigns, and replacement cycles.
- Evidence level: inferred from standard dealer practice and Deere's dealer-led sales model.

### 2. Solution Configuration, Quotation, and Sales
- Assess equipment and attachment requirements.
- Configure machines, technology packages, precision-ag components, attachments, service plans, and protection products.
- Prepare quotations, negotiate terms, confirm availability, and place orders.
- Coordinate new equipment, used equipment, rental, or replacement options.
- Evidence level: partly evidenced by Deere equipment, financing, dealer, and used-equipment channels; detailed dealer workflow is not public.

### 3. Financing and Commercial Structuring
- Present loan, lease, revolving-credit, and multi-use-account options.
- Support finance calculations and credit applications.
- Coordinate approvals, contracts, payment terms, and customer account setup.
- Support financing for new equipment, used equipment, parts, service, attachments, technology, and seasonal purchases.
- Evidence level: evidenced by John Deere Financial public pages; precise dealer responsibilities vary by market.

### 4. Used Equipment Lifecycle
- Accept or source traded / used equipment.
- Inspect, value, recondition, certify, price, list, and sell used machines.
- Attach financing, protection plans, and precision upgrades where applicable.
- Publish inventory through dealer channels and MachineFinder.
- Evidence level: inspection, certification, listing, financing, protection, and precision-upgrade activities are evidenced; trade-in intake and valuation are reasonable dealer-process inference.

### 5. Order Fulfilment, Machine Preparation, and Handover
- Track order, allocation, shipment, receipt, and delivery readiness.
- Perform pre-delivery inspection, configuration, software / firmware setup, attachments, and machine preparation.
- Register equipment and warranty information.
- Transfer equipment into the customer's account and activate connectivity where authorized.
- Train the customer / operator and complete delivery acceptance.
- Evidence level: registration, account transfer, JDLink activation, equipment setup, and training are evidenced; detailed logistics and pre-delivery workflow are inferred from standard equipment-dealer practice.

### 6. Customer and Digital Onboarding
- Create or support the customer's John Deere account and organization.
- Add equipment, fields, users, and partners.
- Establish the dealer partnership and permissions.
- Activate JDLink connectivity and configure data sharing.
- Help customers use Operations Center, Equipment Mobile, work plans, and remote support.
- Evidence level: directly evidenced by Operations Center public documentation.

### 7. Parts Identification, Planning, and Fulfilment
- Identify parts by equipment PIN, model, catalogue, document, or part number.
- Check stock and availability, create carts, place orders, and manage order history.
- Fulfil through dealer pickup or delivery.
- Manage OEM, remanufactured, maintenance, and alternative parts.
- Build customer-specific onsite parts inventories based on machine fleets.
- Support returns, warranty replacement, and replenishment.
- Evidence level: catalogue, ordering, pickup/delivery, saved-cart/history, product tiers, and dealer-developed onsite inventory strategy are directly evidenced; internal replenishment and returns workflow are inferred.

### 8. Maintenance, Workshop, and Field Service
- Receive service request or connected-machine alert.
- Triage urgency, machine condition, warranty status, skills, parts, and location.
- Schedule workshop or field technician work.
- Diagnose, estimate, approve, repair, test, document, and close work.
- Perform planned maintenance, inspections, upgrades, software updates, and service-plan work.
- Manage technician capacity, parts allocation, service history, and customer communication.
- Evidence level: maintenance, inspections, remote diagnosis, technician dispatch, software updates, and service plans are evidenced; detailed work-order stages are industry-standard inference.

### 9. Connected Support and Proactive Uptime Management
- Obtain customer permission and establish machine connectivity.
- Monitor machine alerts, utilization, engine hours, fuel, and idle time.
- Use Expert Alerts to identify potential failures.
- Connect remotely for diagnostics and perform remote software updates.
- Determine required technician skills and likely parts before dispatch.
- Contact the customer, plan intervention, dispatch service, and verify resolution.
- Evidence level: directly evidenced by Connected Support and Operations Center pages.

### 10. Warranty, Protection Plan, and Recall Management
- Register equipment and warranty.
- Verify coverage, service plan, serial number, purchase details, and eligibility.
- Capture diagnosis, labor, parts, and supporting evidence.
- Submit and track warranty claims and approvals.
- Replace covered parts, execute repairs, and maintain audit records.
- Sell and administer extended warranties and maintenance / protection plans.
- Identify affected equipment and execute recall or product-improvement actions.
- Evidence level: registration, warranties, protection plans, covered repair, and dealer warranty systems are evidenced; detailed claims and recall workflow is reasonable inference.

### 11. Precision Agriculture and Operational Support
- Configure customer organization, equipment, fields, team, and connectivity.
- Help create and transmit work plans for tillage, seeding, application, and harvest.
- Monitor fleet and field activity.
- Support data transfer, documentation, analysis, and collaboration.
- Train customers and support adoption of precision-ag capabilities.
- Recommend upgrades and optimize use of existing equipment / technology.
- Evidence level: directly evidenced by Operations Center and used-equipment precision-upgrade pages.

### 12. Installed-Base and Customer-Success Management
- Maintain a view of customer fleet, utilization, service condition, warranty, subscriptions, and lifecycle stage.
- Plan proactive service, seasonal readiness, renewals, upgrades, replacement, and trade-in opportunities.
- Review customer value, uptime, support history, and adoption of connected / digital services.
- Evidence level: fleet monitoring, proactive service, service plans, and upgrades are evidenced; integrated customer-success process is an operating-model inference.

### 13. Dealer-to-Deere Coordination
- Use dealer-specific platforms, technical documentation, warranty systems, and specialist services.
- Escalate complex technical issues.
- Coordinate parts, warranty, product support, software, training, and platform issues with Deere.
- Apply Deere standards, campaigns, product updates, and service guidance locally.
- Evidence level: existence of Dealer Center, Warranty System, Expert Services, technical resources, and connected-support tooling is public; internal process detail is not public.

## Implications for Dealer Channel AI Solutions
- The highest-value AI opportunities sit across process handoffs, not only within individual tasks.
- Service triage can combine alerts, service history, warranty, parts, technician skills, and customer priority.
- Parts assistance can improve identification, substitution, availability, and demand forecasting.
- Technician knowledge assistants can ground answers in approved manuals, service history, and technical guidance.
- Warranty assistance can extract evidence, check completeness, classify claims, and flag exceptions while preserving human approval.
- Sales and configuration copilots can support needs discovery, option comparison, quote preparation, and next-best action.
- Digital onboarding assistants can guide account, equipment, connectivity, permissions, and Operations Center setup.
- Customer-success analytics can identify underused capabilities, service risk, upgrade opportunities, and renewal needs.
- Workflow orchestration can coordinate dealer, customer, Deere, parts, finance, and service actions with explicit ownership and escalation.
- Governance must cover customer consent, equipment and operational data access, explainability, security, auditability, and human decision rights.

## Manufacturing Context Relevance
- Manufacturing environments often involve ERP, engineering, product, operational, data, and integration complexity.
- AI opportunities may relate to operations, engineering productivity, knowledge reuse, analytics, predictive insights, and workflow automation.
- Governance matters because AI solutions must be reliable, explainable enough for use, secure, and operationally maintainable.
