## Applied AI Leadership Perspective

Source:
- Entrepreneur, "AI Won't Replace Leaders - But It Will Expose Weak Ones. Here's How," 2026-06-17.

Relevant principles:
- AI adoption is a leadership and operating-model challenge, not only a technology implementation.
- Automating a broken workflow makes the broken workflow move faster; redesign should come before scale.
- Leaders should remove unnecessary approvals and move informed decisions closer to the work.
- Engineering managers should design work around clarity, accountability, judgment, and trust rather than acting as information conduits.
- AI governance must define ownership, human oversight, decision rights, and accountability.
- Effective leaders combine enough technical fluency to challenge solutions with the judgment to prioritize business value.
- Team trust and capability development matter because adoption fails when AI is treated only as a workforce-reduction mechanism.

Application to this role:
- Expanding the Dealer Channel AI Platform to Europe should not be treated as a technical rollout alone. European dealer workflows, decision points, business needs, adoption constraints, and support models must be understood.
- The Engineering Manager should help the team identify where AI or automation genuinely improves dealer operations and where process simplification is required first.
- Collaboration with Product Managers, Architects, UX, Engineering Leads, and supporting teams should result in explicit decision rights, requirements, design standards, and human oversight.
- Team leadership should focus on clear outcomes, engineering discipline, coaching, and removing delivery friction.
- Platform performance should be considered alongside adoption, operational value, maintainability, trust, and cost.

Evidence from my experience:
- OnePlan was built from ground zero with delivery, security, release, environment, and stakeholder governance.
- Standard-release templates, fewer approvals, SLAs, and secured resources improved release cadence by addressing decision and resource bottlenecks.
- Operating-model work across BISON, SIERA, NEXUS, and SAP SAC redesigned roles, processes, structures, and hierarchies rather than adding technology to unchanged work.
- Agile ceremonies, testing governance, service monitoring, and vulnerability remediation show practical accountability and human oversight.
- More than 10 years of line management, coaching, performance management, career development, and hiring support the people-development side of AI adoption.

#### 22. How do you attract and retain engineering talent?

### Dealer and AI Solution Questions

#### 24. What do you understand about John Deere dealer processes?
From public information, I see the dealer operation as an equipment lifecycle rather than just sales. It covers customer needs and configuration, financing, delivery and digital onboarding, then parts, maintenance, connected support, warranty, precision-ag services, upgrades and replacement. The most valuable AI opportunities are often in the handoffs between customer, machine, service, parts, warranty and operational data.

#### 25. Where could AI create value for dealers?
I would start with high-volume decisions where information is fragmented. Examples include service triage using alerts, history, warranty, parts and technician availability; technician knowledge assistance grounded in approved documentation; warranty evidence checking; parts identification and forecasting; customer onboarding; and identifying service or upgrade opportunities. The use case should be selected based on measurable dealer and customer value, not novelty.

#### 26. Which use case would you investigate first?
Proactive service triage looks promising because John Deere already has connected-machine monitoring, remote diagnostics and Expert Alerts. The opportunity would be to help the dealer turn an alert into an action by combining severity, service history, warranty, likely parts, technician capability and customer priority. I would validate this with dealers before assuming it is the right first use case.

#### 27. How would you prioritize AI use cases?
I would assess business value, frequency, decision pain, data readiness, technical feasibility, operational risk, adoption effort and reusability across dealers. I would also check whether the underlying process is stable enough to automate. A use case with impressive technology but unclear ownership or no measurable outcome should not be prioritized.

#### 28. How would you expand an existing AI platform to Europe?
I would begin with discovery across a representative set of dealers and countries. The objective would be to identify the common process core, local variants, system dependencies, language needs, regulations, consent requirements and adoption constraints. I would then select a limited pilot, define measures and feedback loops, and scale reusable components while keeping local configuration separate from the platform core.

#### 29. How would you avoid automating a broken process?
I would map the current workflow, decisions, approvals, exceptions, information sources and handoffs before designing the AI solution. We should remove unnecessary activity and clarify ownership first. My release-improvement work followed this pattern: document the process, identify bottlenecks, simplify approvals, establish SLAs and secure resources before adding more tooling.

#### 30. How would you manage AI risk and governance?
Governance should be proportionate to the impact of the decision. I would define approved data, access, purpose, ownership, human review, audit logging, monitoring, fallback, and escalation. Customer consent and equipment/operational data access are particularly important in the dealer context. High-impact decisions should remain explainable and accountable to a person.

#### 31. How would you build dealer and employee trust in AI?
I would involve users early and be clear about the problem being solved, what data is used, what the system recommends, and where the person remains responsible. The first releases should solve visible problems and allow feedback and correction. Adoption will be weak if people see AI as something imposed on them or as an unspoken headcount exercise.

#### 32. How would you measure success?
The measures depend on the selected process. For service, I would consider time from alert to action, diagnostic time, first-time fix rate, machine downtime and technician productivity. For parts: identification accuracy, fill rate and urgent orders. Across the platform: adoption, active use, recommendation acceptance, exception rate, reliability, cost and dealer/customer satisfaction.

#### 33. What would you do in your first 90 days?
First I would understand the team, existing platform, roadmap, architecture, delivery practices and current commitments. In parallel I would meet product, architecture, UX, support and representative European dealer stakeholders to understand the operating processes and local variation. I would then clarify priorities, risks, measures and decision rights, address immediate delivery blockers, and agree a practical plan for the first European pilot or expansion milestone.
